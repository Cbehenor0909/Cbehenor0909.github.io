A GUIDED SEQUENCE

Active Robotics Control

Build the chain from robot configuration and force mapping to sampled feedback and compliant interaction.

Functional

Start with physical geometry, then identify the states, forces and delays in the feedback loop. Explain a controller’s input and output units before calculating gains. Return to the Controls concept map when you need pole, frequency or observer analysis.

The paper and review guide adds published interaction-control architectures and reading questions beside these foundational calculations.

The cart exploration uses a synthetic target and one linear degree of freedom. The interaction lesson then explains which additional assumptions enter a robot that contacts a person or environment.

  1. 01
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Map joints to task motion and force ↗

    Map joint motion into tool motion, map contact forces back into joint torques, and recognize the task directions lost at a singularity.

  2. 02
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Compute dynamics and actuator commands ↗

    Separate inertia, velocity coupling, gravity and contact loads, then combine inverse-dynamics feedforward with feedback and actuator limits.

  3. 03
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Represent the controlled system ↗

    Choose states, inputs and outputs, convert higher-order ODEs into first-order equations, and linearize about a stated operating point.

  4. 04
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Estimate the accessible state ↗

    Test state access with rank and PBH conditions, place feedback and observer poles, and distinguish full controllability from stabilizability and detectability.

  5. 05
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Account for sampling and latency ↗

    Follow a measurement through sampling, velocity estimation, digital feedback and held actuator effort, and see why delay and saturation change the closed loop.

  6. 06
    Functional · Interactive exploration

    Explore a complete sampled loop ↗

    Follow a synthetic reach through sensing, latency, velocity estimation and force feedback; inspect how the resulting robot motion changes.

  7. 07
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Shape force-motion interaction ↗

    Design the relationship between contact force and motion, distinguish force-command and motion-command implementations, and use energy to explain an ideal passive interaction.

WHO THIS IS FOR

Students connecting control theory to robot motion, actuation and physical interaction.
