A GUIDED SEQUENCE

Human Sensing → Active Robotics Control

Follow human motion through physical representation, sensing, estimation and feedback to robot action and interaction.

Functional

Think of the route as body → signal → calibrated frame → estimated state → reference → controller → actuator → motion. The feedback arrow returns measured motion to the estimator and controller. Every link has its own units, timing and assumptions.

Use the paper and review guide to connect this sequence to published methods and evidence. Reviews organize a field; primary studies establish results for their own models, tasks and evaluation protocols.

The complete route connects illustrated lessons with existing COM/IMU/kinematics models and a new sampled-loop exploration. Use Human Sensing Foundations for a closer look at the measurement side, or Active Robotics Control to focus on action. Human Balance adds contact and recoverability context.

Carry one question through the route

Suppose a human reach supplies a robot position reference. What quantity does the sensor provide? Which rotation defines the body frame? How old is the sample when the controller consumes it? Is velocity measured or differentiated? What force does the actuator actually apply after saturation? Answer those questions before interpreting a tracking curve.

What completion of this route means

All listed pages are available, with assumptions, calculations, figures and review questions. It is a teaching sequence without personal progress tracking. Its synthetic demonstrations and local software checks do not establish anatomical calibration, participant outcomes or hardware performance.

  1. 01
    Functional · Interactive exploration

    Understand body motion and mass distribution ↗

    From simple objects to human segments, calculate mass-weighted position, velocity and acceleration, then connect the same quantities to OpenSim.

  2. 02
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Identify contact forces and pressure center ↗

    Replace distributed ground contact by a resultant force, locate its center of pressure, and keep force-plate origins and sign conventions explicit.

  3. 03
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Choose and interpret human sensors ↗

    Choose sensing modalities by the quantities they observe, align frames and clocks, and separate raw channels from estimated motion and control states.

  4. 04
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Establish the sensor-to-segment transform ↗

    Define a sensor mounting transform, recover segment orientation, and distinguish an anatomical frame from a convenient sensor frame.

  5. 05
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Estimate orientation ↗

    Integrate gyroscope rates with explicit rotation conventions, understand accelerometer specific force, and explain why fusion needs observable reference directions.

  6. 06
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Form relative joint motion ↗

    Combine calibrated segment orientations and angular velocities in a common frame, then explain the additional conventions needed for anatomical joint angles.

  7. 07
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Handle samples and uncertain state ↗

    Follow sampling, filtering, differentiation and model-based estimation, with explicit noise assumptions and a computed scalar Kalman update.

  8. 08
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Map robot motion and forces ↗

    Map joint motion into tool motion, map contact forces back into joint torques, and recognize the task directions lost at a singularity.

  9. 09
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Account for robot dynamics and actuation ↗

    Separate inertia, velocity coupling, gravity and contact loads, then combine inverse-dynamics feedforward with feedback and actuator limits.

  10. 10
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Implement the timing model of feedback ↗

    Follow a measurement through sampling, velocity estimation, digital feedback and held actuator effort, and see why delay and saturation change the closed loop.

  11. 11
    Functional · Interactive exploration

    Inspect the sensing-to-control loop ↗

    Follow a synthetic reach through sensing, latency, velocity estimation and force feedback; inspect how the resulting robot motion changes.

  12. 12
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Interpret compliant physical interaction ↗

    Design the relationship between contact force and motion, distinguish force-command and motion-command implementations, and use energy to explain an ideal passive interaction.

WHO THIS IS FOR

Learners connecting biomechanics and sensing with active or assistive robotics.
