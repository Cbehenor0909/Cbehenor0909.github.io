A GUIDED SEQUENCE

Dynamics Qualifying Exam Study Path

Build oral-exam explanations from vector language through kinematics, inertia, forces, energy and equations of motion.

Functional

This sequence reorganizes the nine groups in the supplied 2024 Dynamics concept outline into ten connected lessons. The concept map traces all 37 source topic entries to their lessons. Seven reading lessons complement the three existing interactive lessons.

Use each lesson in three passes

  1. Explain: State the concept and assumptions aloud before looking at the equation.
  2. Calculate: Reproduce the worked example, including units and frame labels.
  3. Transfer: Change a force, constraint, axis or mass distribution and predict which terms change.

A useful oral answer names the system boundary, reference frame, geometry, coordinates, unknowns and governing law. It also explains why a term vanishes instead of deleting it without justification.

Connect the methods

Use the pendulum and rigid-body examples to compare force balance with energy methods. The final lesson develops Lagrange’s method in detail and relates it to Newton-Euler and D’Alembert formulations; the numerical-integration section addresses accuracy and convergence. The source outline asks students to pick one derivation method, so depth in one method is a sensible study priority while retaining the vocabulary of the others.

Continue to the Dynamics-to-Controls bridge once you can derive a physical equation and specify its inputs and outputs. These pages provide study material and software demonstrations; the historical topic list supplies the organization, rather than a guarantee about a future exam’s questions.

Reference books named in the outline

  • Paul Mitiguy, Dynamics of Mechanical, Aerospace, and Biomechanical Systems (2020).
  • Jerry H. Ginsberg, Advanced Engineering Dynamics, second edition (1998).
  • Thomas R. Kane and David A. Levinson, Dynamics: Theory and Applications (1985), available through Cornell’s repository.
  • Leonard Meirovitch, Introduction to Dynamics and Control (1985).

Each lesson also links to primary lecture notes for the specific derivation. Use a consistent notation convention when moving between references.

  1. 01
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Vectors and frame-aware differentiation ↗

    Separate a geometric vector from its components, transform between bases, and differentiate vectors when the basis itself moves.

  2. 02
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Coordinates and degrees of freedom ↗

    Count independent configuration variables, express constraints, and map joint motion into Cartesian position, velocity and acceleration.

  3. 03
    Functional · Interactive exploration

    Transport theorem on a turntable ↗

    One motion, two observers. Use a bug on a rotating turntable to understand the transport theorem and the five-term acceleration formula.

  4. 04
    Functional · Interactive exploration

    Recursive spatial kinematics ↗

    Calculate position, velocity, acceleration, angular velocity, and angular acceleration along three spatial links with sliding at the endpoint.

  5. 05
    Functional · Interactive exploration

    Centroids and mass centers ↗

    From simple objects to human segments, calculate mass-weighted position, velocity and acceleration, then connect the same quantities to OpenSim.

  6. 06
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Inertia and axis changes ↗

    Build inertia from a mass distribution, find the inertia about an axis, and transform or shift its matrix without losing frame and point labels.

  7. 07
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Forces and equilibrium ↗

    Build a free-body diagram, reduce a force system to a force and couple, and check equilibrium against the actual contact constraints.

  8. 08
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Newton-Euler particle and rigid-body dynamics ↗

    Relate external forces and moments to motion for particles, rigid bodies and interconnected systems, with explicit inertial-frame assumptions.

  9. 09
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Energy and angular momentum ↗

    Decide which conservation law applies by inspecting external forces, moments and power, then use energy and momentum to check a dynamic model.

  10. 10
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Derive and integrate equations of motion ↗

    Derive a mechanical model using Lagrange's equations, compare Newton–Euler and D'Alembert formulations, and check numerical integration against physics.

WHO THIS IS FOR

Students preparing for an analytical dynamics oral exam or rebuilding mechanics fundamentals.

Before you begin

  • Single-variable and multivariable calculus
  • Basic linear algebra
  • Introductory mechanics
