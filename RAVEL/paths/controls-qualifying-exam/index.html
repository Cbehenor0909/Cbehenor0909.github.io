A GUIDED SEQUENCE

Controls Qualifying Exam Study Path

Describe one dynamical system in time, state space, Laplace and frequency domains, then design and check feedback.

Functional

All nine entries in the supplied Controls concept outline have an available lesson in this sequence. The concept map retains the outline’s organization. Each lesson includes an original worked calculation and oral checks; static lessons are labeled so that their role is clear beside the site’s interactive models.

Carry one system across the representations

After the state-space step, try Phase Portraits to compare trajectories, time traces and eigenvalues. Change the initial state before changing the coefficients, then distinguish a stable node, focus, center and saddle. This interactive companion supplements the nine outline steps.

Continue to Stability and Performance to vary a feedback gain while comparing poles, a Routh first column and zero-state versus initial-state responses. Then use the Root Locus explorer to study gain-dependent pole paths and test a desired point with the angle and magnitude conditions.

For a mass-spring-damper system, derive the ODE from forces, choose position and velocity as states, calculate the transfer function under zero initial conditions, and inspect poles and frequency response. Then design feedback and calculate the new poles. For a double integrator, compare direct pole placement with the controllability test.

A repeatable oral-answer structure

  1. Define the model, signal directions, input, output and initial conditions.
  2. State the applicable theorem and its hypotheses.
  3. Perform a short calculation or sketch the qualitative response.
  4. Check stability, units, limiting behavior and the stated performance target.

For example, a final-value calculation requires its pole condition; a transfer function alone can hide internal modes; and a gain-margin number needs a declared loop and feedback convention. These qualifications are part of the answer.

Read the Dynamics-to-Controls bridge alongside this path to connect the two outlines. The original topic list guides coverage, and the lessons provide educational analysis rather than a prediction of future exam questions.

Reference books named in the outline

  • Farid Golnaraghi and Benjamin C. Kuo, Automatic Control Systems, tenth edition.
  • Katsuhiko Ogata, Modern Control Engineering.
  • Bernard Friedland, Control System Design: An Introduction to State-Space Methods.

The outline accepts different editions for the latter two references. Lesson references provide primary lecture/textbook sources for particular claims and calculations.

  1. 01
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Linear algebra and ODE foundations ↗

    Connect eigenvalues, eigenvectors, rank and the matrix exponential to the behavior of a linear dynamical system.

  2. 02
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Build a state-space model ↗

    Choose states, inputs and outputs, convert higher-order ODEs into first-order equations, and linearize about a stated operating point.

  3. 03
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Laplace transforms and theorem conditions ↗

    Solve initial-value problems with the unilateral Laplace transform and apply convolution, complex shifting and value theorems under their conditions.

  4. 04
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Impulse response and transfer functions ↗

    Derive a zero-state input/output model, connect it to impulse response and state space, and identify what pole-zero cancellation can hide.

  5. 05
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Feedback interconnections ↗

    Derive interconnected transfer functions from signal equations, then use Mason's rule while keeping feedback signs and non-touching loops explicit.

  6. 06
    Functional · Interactive exploration

    Stability and performance checks ↗

    Move a feedback gain across its stability boundary, compare poles with forced and initial-state responses, and connect Routh tests to performance and sensitivity.

  7. 07
    Functional · Interactive exploration

    Root-locus analysis and design ↗

    Construct six root loci step by step, explore gain limits and departure angles, then connect candidate-pole tests to the cubic step response.

  8. 08
    Functional · Interactive exploration

    Bode analysis and loop shaping ↗

    Build six Bode plots factor by factor, compare sketches with exact curves, inspect all gain crossovers, and connect margins to lead and lag design.

  9. 09
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Controllability ↗

    Test state access with rank and PBH conditions, place feedback and observer poles, and distinguish full controllability from stabilizability and detectability.

WHO THIS IS FOR

Students preparing for a controls oral exam or connecting mechanical models to control design.
