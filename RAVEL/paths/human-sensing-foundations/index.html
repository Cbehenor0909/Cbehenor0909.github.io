A GUIDED SEQUENCE

Human Sensing Foundations

From raw human-motion signals to calibrated, time-aligned segment and state estimates.

Functional

Carry the same frame and time convention through every step. For each output, explain which quantity was measured directly, which was estimated, and which assumptions made that estimate possible.

Read the paper and review guide alongside this path. It links systematic reviews and representative method papers to specific lessons, with selected original figures and prompts for interpreting their evidence.

Each step is available as an illustrated reading lesson or an existing interactive model. Compare the schematic calculations with the declared sensor-model assumptions before moving to robot feedback.

  1. 01
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Choose the measured quantity ↗

    Choose sensing modalities by the quantities they observe, align frames and clocks, and separate raw channels from estimated motion and control states.

  2. 02
    Functional · Interactive exploration

    Read an IMU in its own frame ↗

    Attach a virtual IMU to an upper arm, move the segment, and follow how coordinate transforms produce sensor readings.

  3. 03
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Calibrate the sensor to the segment ↗

    Define a sensor mounting transform, recover segment orientation, and distinguish an anatomical frame from a convenient sensor frame.

  4. 04
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Estimate orientation and understand drift ↗

    Integrate gyroscope rates with explicit rotation conventions, understand accelerometer specific force, and explain why fusion needs observable reference directions.

  5. 05
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Compute relative segment motion ↗

    Combine calibrated segment orientations and angular velocities in a common frame, then explain the additional conventions needed for anatomical joint angles.

  6. 06
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Filter signals and estimate state ↗

    Follow sampling, filtering, differentiation and model-based estimation, with explicit noise assumptions and a computed scalar Kalman update.

WHO THIS IS FOR

Students and researchers interpreting motion capture, IMUs, contact forces and human signals.
