A GUIDED SEQUENCE

Human Balance Foundations

From the ground beneath us to the mechanics of staying upright.

Functional

Each step introduces one idea before connecting it to the next. Explore contact and COM, read a force-plate example, then reason about moving balance, recovery and measurement.

All six steps are available. The order is a teaching sequence, not a claim that every concept is a strict mathematical prerequisite. Use each lesson’s assumptions and oral questions to check understanding. There is no account, saved progress, or completion tracking.

  1. 01
    Functional · Interactive exploration

    Base of Support ↗

    How ground contact defines the region beneath a supporting body.

  2. 02
    Functional · Interactive exploration

    Center of Mass ↗

    From simple objects to human segments, calculate mass-weighted position, velocity and acceleration, then connect the same quantities to OpenSim.

  3. 03
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Center of Pressure ↗

    Replace distributed ground contact by a resultant force, locate its center of pressure, and keep force-plate origins and sign conventions explicit.

  4. 04
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Dynamic balance and capture ↗

    Add COM velocity to support geometry, derive the constant-height inverted-pendulum model, and interpret a signed margin of stability within its assumptions.

  5. 05
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Walking ↗

    Track loaded support through a walking cycle, distinguish future foot placement from current contact, and calculate how recovery timing changes a model capture target.

  6. 06
    Functional · Reading lesson

    Measuring human motion ↗

    Choose sensing modalities by the quantities they observe, align frames and clocks, and separate raw channels from estimated motion and control states.

WHO THIS IS FOR

Students and researchers beginning to connect mechanics with human movement.

Before you begin

  • Basic vectors and geometry
  • A willingness to distinguish a model from a measurement
