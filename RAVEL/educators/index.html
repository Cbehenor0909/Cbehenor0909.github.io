Classroom activities

Foundations

Trace motion through a chain

Learning objective Recursively compute r, v, a, ω, and α along a chain from a fixed origin.

Predict
If the joints are locked, can a sliding endpoint still have relative velocity?
Experiment
Select the final link, lock the joints and compare the endpoint velocity with sliding enabled and disabled.
Explain
Discuss the result

Locking the joints removes angular transport. An enabled extension can still change link length and move the endpoint along the link.

Apply
List the joint angles and extension coordinates needed to describe a telescoping robot arm.

Connect damping to a phase trajectory

Learning objective Distinguish a direction field, an individual trajectory and time traces.

Predict
For positive stiffness, what will happen to a closed orbit if damping is added?
Experiment
Choose the center preset. Keep stiffness and the initial state fixed, add a small positive damping coefficient and compare both time traces.
Explain
Discuss the result

Damping dissipates mechanical energy. For light damping the orbit spirals toward equilibrium as the oscillation amplitude decays.

Apply
Describe one feature of a free-decay recording that could distinguish an undamped model from a lightly damped one.
Open Phase Portraits · Read Motion in State Space →
Compare two observers of the same slider

Learning objective Distinguish an observer derivative from a vector's components in a chosen basis.

Predict
A slider moves outward at constant speed on a spinning disk. Which observer sees a straight path?
Experiment
Scrub time in both views. Set the relative speed to zero while keeping the disk spinning and compare the acceleration terms.
Explain
Discuss the result

Outward motion in the rotating frame produces a Coriolis contribution in the inertial frame. With zero relative speed, that term vanishes while centripetal acceleration remains.

Apply
Identify the observer and the frame before interpreting an acceleration trace from a rotating mechanism.
Open Transport Formula · Bug on a Turntable →

Observe

Rotate the mounting on a stationary arm

Learning objective Explain why a supported, stationary accelerometer reads about 1 g while the gyroscope reads zero.

Predict
Which sensor channels should change when a stationary IMU is remounted by 90°?
Experiment
Compare the rest and 90° mount presets. Record all three accelerometer and gyroscope components in each mounting.
Explain
Discuss the result

The support specific-force vector has the same magnitude in both sensor frames. A mounting rotation redistributes its components. Angular rate stays zero at rest.

Apply
Before collecting a trial, document the mounting axes, units and expected stationary readings.

Act

Move the crossover with gain

Learning objective Evaluate logarithmic magnitude and phase.

Predict
What happens to magnitude and phase at a fixed frequency when a positive gain doubles?
Experiment
Use the first example and set the probe to 1 rad/s. Compare two gains in a two-to-one ratio and inspect the crossover frequency separately.
Explain
Discuss the result

At the fixed probe the magnitude rises by about 6.02 dB and phase is unchanged. The crossover moves, so its phase margin can change.

Apply
Describe why a phase measurement at the old crossover cannot establish the new phase margin.

Separate gain from response time

Learning objective Apply root-locus angle and magnitude conditions.

Predict
If only the observation time changes, will the selected poles move?
Experiment
At fixed gain, sweep response time. Then hold the time cursor still and choose the imaginary-axis crossing.
Explain
Discuss the result

Gain changes the characteristic polynomial and its roots. Sampling the response at another time evaluates the same dynamics at a different instant.

Apply
When reviewing a design plot, identify which variable traces each curve before comparing its axes.
Open Root Locus →
Isolate one source of tracking error

Learning objective Trace the signals from a synthetic human target to robot motion.

Predict
With gains and sampling rate fixed, how might older measurements change the cart’s tracking?
Experiment
Begin with the nominal loop. Increase sensor latency alone and compare position, force and RMS tracking error for the same reach.
Explain
Discuss the result

The controller uses older samples while the cart continues moving. The observed effect depends on the chosen gains, sampling rate and force limit.

Apply
For laboratory onboarding, draw the measurement-to-actuation chain and identify where timestamps would be needed to estimate delay.

Find the feedback stability boundary

Learning objective Distinguish asymptotic internal, Lyapunov and BIBO stability.

Predict
When gain passes through six, what should happen to an oscillation after a unit step?
Experiment
Start at K = 6. Compare the near-boundary and unstable presets. Check the poles, response and Routh first column.
Explain
Discuss the result

At six the polynomial factors into (s + 3)(s² + 2). The imaginary-axis pair produces a persistent mode. Above six the pair moves into the right half-plane.

Apply
Explain why a short response trace near the boundary can be insufficient for assigning stability.

Embodiment

What disappears at toe-off?

Learning objective Identify the contacts that contribute to the base of support.

Predict
Sketch the support boundary just before the right foot leaves the ground.
Experiment
Advance gait phase from 9% to 10%. Mark the active contact patches on both sketches.
Explain
Discuss the result

The hull spans both feet at 9%. At 10% only the left footprint contributes. The inter-foot region disappears even though neither footprint moved.

Apply
Before interpreting a gait recording, identify how contact was detected. What additional measurements would help you judge dynamic balance?
Open Base of Support →
Move the center without moving the masses

Learning objective Compute COM for point masses and continuous distributions.

Predict
For two fixed point masses, which way will the center move if the second mass doubles?
Experiment
Choose two point masses. Hold separation fixed, record the center position, then double the second mass within the slider range.
Explain
Discuss the result

Each position is weighted by its fraction of total mass. The center moves toward the heavier mass and stays between the points.

Apply
Explain why the geometric midpoint of two body segments can be a poor estimate of their combined center of mass.
Open Center of Mass · Objects, Segments and Motion →

Teach from a worked example

In the beam equilibrium lesson, give students the two support locations and the two downward loads. Ask them to predict which support carries more load, draw the free-body diagram, and calculate the vertical reactions before revealing the solution.

Discuss the result

The loads total 140 N. Their moment about the pin is 190 N·m, so the roller 2 m away supplies 95 N and the pin supplies 45 N vertically. As a transfer question, move the 100 N load toward the pin and ask which reaction decreases.

Laboratory onboarding

Give a new lab member a drawing of a stationary upper arm and its sensor mount. Have them label the segment and sensor axes, predict the three accelerometer components and check the prediction in the virtual IMU.

Next, use the sensor-to-segment calibration lesson to specify the rotation needed to express the reading in the segment frame. Ask for a one-page record of the frame convention, units, calibration pose and one source of mounting error.

For a literature discussion, choose a study from the paper guide. Identify its reference instrument and outcome measure. Which claim depends on measured data beyond the classroom model?
