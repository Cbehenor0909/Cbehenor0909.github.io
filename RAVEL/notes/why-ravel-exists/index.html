Why RAVEL exists

A place to connect physical intuition, explicit models, and questions we can explore.

visual intuition · teaching · models

Start with a question

A diagram becomes useful when it helps us ask a more precise question. What is moving? Relative to which frame? What is measured, and what is assumed?

RAVEL is a place to keep these questions connected to small, reusable explanations. Its scope reaches from mechanics and sensing to control, human movement, and robotics. It begins with one exploration, not a finished textbook.

Make the model visible

An interactive model should reveal its assumptions as clearly as its behavior. A slider needs a physical meaning. A plotted line needs a label. A useful explanation also tells us where the model stops being useful.

The Base of Support exploration is a small example: change which foot contacts the ground and observe how the support region changes. The geometry is useful; it is not a prediction of whether a moving person will fall.

Build connections slowly

Small modules can become a teaching sequence, a journal-club explanation, or a starting point for a research discussion. Notes provide room for derivations and unresolved questions without requiring every idea to become an interactive application.

The aim is a collection that remains understandable and reusable over time. One clear question, one explicit model, and one meaningful interaction are enough to begin.
