Papers and Reviews · Human Sensing to Robotics

Read five reviews and five representative method studies alongside the lessons, with original licensed figures and questions about their evidence.

Human sensing · Robotics · systematic review · scoping review · IMU · OpenSim · OpenSense · OpenCap · impedance · admittance · papers

Read a review and a method together

A review maps approaches, evaluation choices and gaps. A primary paper specifies a method and reports evidence for a particular model, task, dataset or device. Read both beside the Human Sensing path and Robotics Control path. This is a selected reading route rather than an exhaustive or continuously updated literature search.

Start with these five reviews

  1. Poitras et al. (2019), systematic review. Validity and Reliability of Wearable Sensors for Joint Angle Estimation. Sensors 19(7), 1555. Read beside relative joint kinematics. Ask which joint, task, reference and metric support each validity or reliability claim. Repeatability and accuracy answer different questions.

  2. Pacher et al. (2020), systematic review. Sensor-to-Segment Calibration Methodologies for Lower-Body Kinematic Analysis with Inertial Sensors. Sensors 20(11), 3322. Read beside mounting calibration. Compare manual, static, functional and anatomical assumptions; heterogeneous evaluation protocols complicate method ranking.

  3. Wade, Needham, McGuigan and Bilzon (2022), scoping review. Applications and limitations of current markerless motion capture methods for clinical gait biomechanics. PeerJ 10, e12995. Read beside human motion sensing. Identify how camera configuration, motion reconstruction and anatomical interpretation enter a reported result.

  4. Abu-Dakka and Saveriano (2020), review. Variable Impedance Control and Learning—A Review. Frontiers in Robotics and AI 7, 590681. Read beside impedance/admittance. Locate which parameters vary, what drives adaptation and which stability assumptions survive the change.

  5. Hogan (2022), review. Contact and Physical Interaction. Annual Review of Control, Robotics, and Autonomous Systems 5, 179–203. Advanced reading for interaction control: connect apparent dynamics, actuation, contact and energy exchange before interpreting a compliance command.

Questions to carry back to the course

  • What quantity is directly observed, and what is inferred through a model?
  • Which calibration, frame and timestamp conventions connect the channels?
  • Does an error metric describe sensor orientation, fit residual, joint angle, moment or tracking?
  • Which population, task, reference method and test duration support the result?
  • For interaction control, which dynamics and input power were actually evaluated?

Return to the complete learning sequence. Published figures retain their study context; they are not measurements from RAVEL and do not validate its teaching simulations.
