About RAVEL

Robotics · Analysis · Visualization · Embodiment · Learning

Purpose and scientific scope

RAVEL is a personal research and teaching platform, intended to grow throughout a PhD and beyond. It connects physical intuition, mathematical representation, visual exploration, and eventually measurement.

Its scope spans mechanics, dynamics, control, sensing, human movement, assistive systems, and robotics. The four domains offer different perspectives on connected ideas.

How the content is organized

Start with a domain for a broad scientific perspective or a topic collection for a group of connected ideas. Each module addresses one question. A learning path puts those same modules in a teaching sequence.

From physical intuition to mathematical understanding

Begin with a diagram, predict what will change, then test that prediction. Use the equations to explain the result and identify where the model applies. The educator guide turns these steps into classroom experiments and laboratory introductions.

Using the collection

Use an available module as a starting point for a mentoring conversation, a concept review, or a teaching demonstration. Check its status and assumptions before relying on it. “Functional” describes working software. Scientific review and teaching trials have separate status labels.

Explore includes 10 interactive modules. Their analytical and synthetic models illustrate physical relationships. Reading lessons identify published experimental results and their sources separately.

About the author

RAVEL is an independently maintained doctoral research and teaching project. The project note describes its origins and the author’s approach to scientific models.
