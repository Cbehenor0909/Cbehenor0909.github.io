Meet the kinematic chain

NNN is the fixed inertial frame, and OOO is fixed. The three links are O→AO\to AO→A, A→BA\to BA→B, and B→PB\to PB→P, with local frames AAA, BBB, and CCC. Distinguish points A/BA/BA/B from the frames with the same names: frame AAA has its origin at OOO, frame BBB at point AAA, and frame CCC at point BBB.

  1. Frame AAA rotates relative to NNN about n^z\hat{\mathbf{n}}_zn^z​ through q1q_1q1​. Link 1 extends along a^x\hat{\mathbf{a}}_xa^x​ with length L1L_1L1​.

Explore in this order

  • Start with r\mathbf{r}r: Select links 1, 2, and 3. Distinguish the total position r\mathbf{r}r from OOO from the displacement ρ\boldsymbol{\rho}ρ measured from the selected link’s origin.
  • Then inspect v\mathbf{v}v: For link 3, select the origin velocity, relative velocity, and ω×ρ\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{\rho}ω×ρ terms. Disabling sliding removes only the relative term; the moving-origin and rotation terms may remain.
  • Then inspect a\mathbf{a}a: Examine each of the five terms. As you move from link 1 to link 3, the preceding endpoint’s total acceleration becomes the next link’s origin term.
  • Inspect ω\boldsymbol{\omega}ω and α\boldsymbol{\alpha}α: Use the “Constant joint rates” preset. All q¨\ddot{q}q¨​ values are zero, yet downstream α\boldsymbol{\alpha}α may be nonzero because the rotation axes move with their parent frames.
  • Lock all joints: Sliding is disabled and q˙\dot{q}q˙​ and q¨\ddot{q}q¨​ are zero, so all velocities and accelerations vanish.

Pause, scrub time, change the view, and express numerical values in the selected local basis. Changing the basis does not change the observer: v\mathbf{v}v and a\mathbf{a}a in the table remain derivatives observed in NNN, while ω\boldsymbol{\omega}ω and α\boldsymbol{\alpha}α describe angular motion relative to NNN.

One transport theorem, applied repeatedly

For any vector X\mathbf{X}X and a frame FFF rotating relative to NNN:

N ⁣dXdt=F ⁣dXdt+ωF/N×X.{}^{N}\!\frac{d\mathbf{X}}{dt} = {}^{F}\!\frac{d\mathbf{X}}{dt} + \boldsymbol{\omega}^{F/N}\times\mathbf{X}.NdtdX​=FdtdX​+ωF/N×X.

For each link, let QQQ be its origin and EEE its endpoint. Write rE=rQ+ρ\mathbf{r}^{E} = \mathbf{r}^{Q} + \boldsymbol{\rho}rE=rQ+ρ, with ρ=ℓf^x\boldsymbol{\rho} = \ell\hat{\mathbf{f}}_xρ=ℓf^x​. The endpoint’s derivatives relative to that link’s frame are

vrel=ℓ˙f^x,arel=ℓ¨f^x.\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{rel}} = \dot{\ell}\hat{\mathbf{f}}_x, \qquad \mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{rel}} = \ddot{\ell}\hat{\mathbf{f}}_x.vrel​=ℓ˙f^x​,arel​=ℓ¨f^x​.

The velocity recursion is

NvE=NvQ+vrel+ω×ρ.{}^{N}\mathbf{v}^{E} = {}^{N}\mathbf{v}^{Q} + \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{rel}} + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{\rho}.NvE=NvQ+vrel​+ω×ρ.

The acceleration recursion is

NaE=NaQ+arel+2ω×vrel+α×ρ+ω×(ω×ρ).\begin{aligned} {}^{N}\mathbf{a}^{E} &= {}^{N}\mathbf{a}^{Q} + \mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{rel}} + 2\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{rel}} \\ &\quad + \boldsymbol{\alpha}\times\boldsymbol{\rho} + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\boldsymbol{\rho}). \end{aligned}NaE​=NaQ+arel​+2ω×vrel​+α×ρ+ω×(ω×ρ).​

Here ω\boldsymbol{\omega}ω is the selected link’s total angular velocity relative to NNN, rather than only its own joint rate; α=N ⁣dωdt\boldsymbol{\alpha} = {}^{N}\!\frac{d\boldsymbol{\omega}}{dt}α=Ndtdω​. Express all right-hand terms in the same basis. The model adds them in NNN, then optionally converts the resulting components to the selected local basis.

Why does angular acceleration require more than adding q̈ terms?

Suppose the current joint’s unit axis e^\hat{\mathbf{e}}e^ is fixed in its parent frame FFF. Then

ωchild=ωparent+q˙e^.\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{child}} = \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{parent}} + \dot{q}\hat{\mathbf{e}}.ωchild​=ωparent​+q˙​e^.

Differentiate with respect to NNN. Since e^\hat{\mathbf{e}}e^ rotates with the parent frame, N ⁣de^dt=ωparent×e^{}^{N}\!\frac{d\hat{\mathbf{e}}}{dt} = \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{parent}}\times\hat{\mathbf{e}}Ndtde^​=ωparent​×e^, giving

αchild=αparent+q¨e^+ωparent×(q˙e^).\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{child}} &= \boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{parent}} + \ddot{q}\hat{\mathbf{e}} \\ &\quad + \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathrm{parent}}\times(\dot{q}\hat{\mathbf{e}}). \end{aligned}αchild​​=αparent​+q¨​e^+ωparent​×(q˙​e^).​

In three dimensions, the double-cross-product contribution points toward the axis through the link origin parallel to ω\boldsymbol{\omega}ω, rather than necessarily toward the origin itself. It reduces to −∥ω∥2ρ-\lVert\boldsymbol{\omega}\rVert^2\boldsymbol{\rho}−∥ω∥2ρ only when ρ\boldsymbol{\rho}ρ is perpendicular to ω\boldsymbol{\omega}ω. The interface labels this term “centripetal,” but evaluates the full double cross product.

Disabling sliding makes the relative and Coriolis terms zero. If the first two links still move, retain the origin term. Contributions from the third link’s own axial roll cancel appropriately in the complete recursion, preserving the motion of an endpoint on that axis. An isolated term does not describe the complete motion.

Calculation conventions and checks

Rotations are right-handed and use column vectors:

RNA=Rz(q1),RNB=RNARy(q2),RNC=RNBRx(q3).\begin{aligned} R^{NA} &= R_z(q_1), \\ R^{NB} &= R^{NA}R_y(q_2), \\ R^{NC} &= R^{NB}R_x(q_3). \end{aligned}RNARNBRNC​=Rz​(q1​),=RNARy​(q2​),=RNBRx​(q3​).​

Position is

rP=L1a^x+L2b^x+ℓ3c^x.\mathbf{r}^{P} = L_1\hat{\mathbf{a}}_x + L_2\hat{\mathbf{b}}_x + \ell_3\hat{\mathbf{c}}_x.rP=L1​a^x​+L2​b^x​+ℓ3​c^x​.

Joint angles are prescribed by

qi(t)=qi0+q˙i0t+12q¨it2,q˙i(t)=q˙i0+q¨it.\begin{aligned} q_i(t) &= q_{i0} + \dot{q}_{i0}t + \tfrac{1}{2}\ddot{q}_it^2, \\ \dot{q}_i(t) &= \dot{q}_{i0} + \ddot{q}_it. \end{aligned}qi​(t)q˙​i​(t)​=qi0​+q˙​i0​t+21​q¨​i​t2,=q˙​i0​+q¨​i​t.​

These checks validate the kinematic implementation, rather than hardware or experimental behavior.

Oral practice

“I start from the fixed frame N and move along the chain. For each link, I add the velocity and acceleration of its moving origin. I use the link’s total angular velocity relative to N, rather than only the joint rate. The relative sliding introduces both relative acceleration and Coriolis acceleration. The angular acceleration also includes the transport of each joint axis by its parent frame.”

Use “Constant joint rates” to explain why link 2 can have nonzero α\boldsymbol{\alpha}α. Disable sliding and identify which of the five endpoint terms vanish. Finally, change q3q_3q3​: predict how the endpoint position and the local basis will respond, then check your prediction.

Check your understanding

  • Recursively compute r, v, a, ω, and α along a chain from a fixed origin.
  • Explain the moving-origin, Coriolis, Euler, and double-cross-product contributions.
  • Distinguish constant joint rates from constant spatial angular velocity.
  • Distinguish derivatives observed in N from components expressed in a local basis.
