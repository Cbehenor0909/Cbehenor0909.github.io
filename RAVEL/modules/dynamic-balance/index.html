Dynamic Balance · Velocity, XCoM and Capture Point

Add COM velocity to support geometry, derive the constant-height inverted-pendulum model, and interpret a signed margin of stability within its assumptions.

intermediateFunctionalReading lesson

Why can the COM lie inside the support region while the current motion still needs a recovery action?

The same position can hide different motion

Imagine two bodies whose COMs project onto the same point between the feet. One is still; the other moves rapidly toward the toes. Their support geometry is identical, but their future motion differs. Dynamic balance needs both position and velocity, together with a model of what contact forces and future steps can do.

A small model with explicit assumptions

Use a point-mass linear inverted pendulum model, or LIPM, with horizontal COM coordinate xxx, constant height h>0h>0h>0, level support and COP coordinate ppp. Gravity is g=9.81 m/s2g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}g=9.81m/s2. Vertical acceleration is zero, so Fz=mgF_z=mgFz​=mg. Neglecting the rate of centroidal angular momentum places the resultant ground force along the line joining COP and COM. Consequently,

Fx=x−phFz,x¨=gh(x−p)=ω02(x−p),ω0=g/h.F_x=\frac{x-p}{h}F_z, \qquad \ddot x=\frac gh(x-p)=\omega_0^2(x-p), \qquad \omega_0=\sqrt{g/h}.Fx​=hx−p​Fz​,x¨=hg​(x−p)=ω02​(x−p),ω0​=g/h​.

ω0\omega_0ω0​ is a model rate in s−1\mathrm{s^{-1}}s−1, not a measured body angular velocity. The sign matters: with COP behind COM, acceleration points forward. Unlike a hanging pendulum, an unsupported deviation grows. COP can only be commanded within feasible loaded contact, with sufficient friction and force capacity.

XCoM exposes the growing mode

Hof and colleagues combine COM position and scaled velocity in the extrapolated center of mass. For this constant-height model,

ξ=x+x˙ω0,ξ˙=ω0(ξ−p).\xi=x+\frac{\dot x}{\omega_0}, \qquad \dot\xi=\omega_0(\xi-p).ξ=x+ω0​x˙​,ξ˙​=ω0​(ξ−p).

x(t)=p+(x(0)−p)e−ω0t.x(t)=p+(x(0)-p)e^{-\omega_0t}.x(t)=p+(x(0)−p)e−ω0​t.

The model then approaches rest above that point. This is the fixed-height, zero-angular-momentum capture point interpretation associated with Pratt and colleagues. More general capture regions depend on additional dynamics and constraints; they need not reduce to one point.

For a horizontal two-dimensional support polygon, state whether the reported margin is a directional boundary distance or a signed Euclidean distance to the nearest boundary. Specify the coordinate frame and evaluation instant. Changing those definitions changes the number.

What the margin leaves out

A negative value says that this particular model cannot capture by placing COP at the current ξ\xiξ inside the current support interval. It does not prove that a human will fall: stepping, changing height, trunk/arm momentum and additional contacts can alter the outcome. A positive value also does not guarantee feasible control when friction, torque, delay or foot loading restrict COP. Neither sign is a clinical threshold.

Gait and Recovery adds moving contacts and step timing. Sampled Feedback Control explains why an estimate that arrives late can change the required action.

Common mistakes

  • Judging moving balance from COM position alone.
  • Treating the height parameter as a universal anatomical constant.
  • Reporting an unsigned distance without saying which support edge matters.
  • Treating model capture as proof of human recovery or controller feasibility.

Oral practice

  1. Predict how doubling forward velocity changes ξ\xiξ at fixed height.
  2. Explain the sign of acceleration when COP is behind COM.
  3. State three assumptions required before interpreting the calculated margin.

Check your understanding

  • Derive horizontal COM acceleration under LIPM assumptions.
  • Compute XCoM from position, velocity and effective height.
  • Define a signed margin with an explicit boundary convention.
  • Separate model capture conditions from human balance claims.
