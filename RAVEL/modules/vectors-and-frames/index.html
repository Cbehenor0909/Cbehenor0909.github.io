Vectors, Frames and Differentiation

Separate a geometric vector from its components, transform between bases, and differentiate vectors when the basis itself moves.

intermediateFunctionalReading lesson

What changes when a vector is described in another frame, and what changes when that frame rotates?

One arrow, several descriptions

A vector is a geometric object with magnitude and direction. A column of three numbers is its representation in a chosen basis. Frame NNN includes an origin and orthonormal axes n^x,n^y,n^z\hat{\mathbf n}_x,\hat{\mathbf n}_y,\hat{\mathbf n}_zn^x​,n^y​,n^z​; frame BBB has its own origin and axes. Re-expressing a free vector needs orientation only. Transforming a point’s position also needs the displacement between origins.

Read the diagram. Follow the blue arrow first, then read its projections on the fixed NNN axes. The tilted BBB axes assign different components to the same arrow. A change of basis preserves its length and direction; a physical rotation would move the arrow. Shared origin and a counterclockwise planar rotation are shown schematically.

Write A=Axn^x+Ayn^y+Azn^z\mathbf A=A_x\hat{\mathbf n}_x+A_y\hat{\mathbf n}_y+A_z\hat{\mathbf n}_zA=Ax​n^x​+Ay​n^y​+Az​n^z​. Add and subtract components only after expressing the vectors in a common basis. In that basis,

A⋅B=AxBx+AyBy+AzBz=∥A∥∥B∥cos⁡γ,A×B=[AyBz−AzByAzBx−AxBzAxBy−AyBx].\begin{aligned} \mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B&=A_xB_x+A_yB_y+A_zB_z =\lVert\mathbf A\rVert\lVert\mathbf B\rVert\cos\gamma,\\ \mathbf A\times\mathbf B&= \begin{bmatrix}A_yB_z-A_zB_y\\A_zB_x-A_xB_z\\A_xB_y-A_yB_x\end{bmatrix}. \end{aligned}A⋅BA×B​=Ax​Bx​+Ay​By​+Az​Bz​=∥A∥∥B∥cosγ,=​Ay​Bz​−Az​By​Az​Bx​−Ax​Bz​Ax​By​−Ay​Bx​​​.​

Use a right-handed basis. Direction cosines and rotations

Define RNBR_{NB}RNB​ to map components from BBB into NNN. Its ijijij entry is n^i⋅b^j\hat{\mathbf n}_i\cdot\hat{\mathbf b}_jn^i​⋅b^j​: each column is one BBB axis expressed in NNN.

[A]N=RNB[A]B,RNB−1=RNBT,det⁡RNB=1.[\mathbf A]_N=R_{NB}[\mathbf A]_B,\qquad R_{NB}^{-1}=R_{NB}^{T},\qquad \det R_{NB}=1.[A]N​=RNB​[A]B​,RNB−1​=RNBT​,detRNB​=1.

These orthogonality and determinant checks distinguish a proper rotation from scaling or reflection. Since RTR=1R^TR=1RTR=1, (Ra)T(Rb)=aTb(R\mathbf a)^T(R\mathbf b)=\mathbf a^T\mathbf b(Ra)T(Rb)=aTb: re-expression preserves dot products and vector magnitudes. Proper rotations also preserve the right-handed cross product; a reflection reverses its orientation. The upper-left block is the 2-D direction-cosine matrix. At θ=90∘\theta=90^\circθ=90∘, a vector with BBB components [1,0,0]T[1,0,0]^T[1,0,0]T has NNN components [0,1,0]T[0,1,0]^T[0,1,0]T. The arrow did not acquire a second physical motion because we changed its description.

In 3-D, an example Euler-angle parameterization is R=Rz(ψ)Ry(θ)Rx(ϕ)R=R_z(\psi)R_y(\theta)R_x(\phi)R=Rz​(ψ)Ry​(θ)Rx​(ϕ), with the rightmost matrix acting first. Specify the axes, multiplication order and mapping direction before using angle values. Rotations generally do not commute. Euler angles describe orientation locally; their three derivatives are generally not the three components of angular velocity. Some angle configurations are singular even though the body’s physical orientation is well defined.

Differentiating a vector function

In a fixed basis, differentiate each component of A(t)\mathbf A(t)A(t). A derivative is another vector; differentiating magnitude alone loses directional changes. For any single observer derivative DDD,

D(A±B)=DA±DB,D(fA)=(Df)A+fDA,D(A⋅B)=(DA)⋅B+A⋅DB,D(A×B)=(DA)×B+A×DB.\begin{aligned} D(\mathbf A\pm\mathbf B)&=D\mathbf A\pm D\mathbf B,\\ D(f\mathbf A)&=(Df)\mathbf A+fD\mathbf A,\\ D(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B)&=(D\mathbf A)\cdot\mathbf B+\mathbf A\cdot D\mathbf B,\\ D(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B)&=(D\mathbf A)\times\mathbf B+\mathbf A\times D\mathbf B. \end{aligned}D(A±B)D(fA)D(A⋅B)D(A×B)​=DA±DB,=(Df)A+fDA,=(DA)⋅B+A⋅DB,=(DA)×B+A×DB.​

Apply the same rule again for higher derivatives. For example, D2(fA)=(D2f)A+2(Df)(DA)+fD2AD^2(f\mathbf A)=(D^2f)\mathbf A+2(Df)(D\mathbf A)+fD^2\mathbf AD2(fA)=(D2f)A+2(Df)(DA)+fD2A. Cross-product order must be retained.

For rotating axes, DNb^i=ωB/N×b^iD_N\hat{\mathbf b}_i=\boldsymbol\omega^{B/N}\times\hat{\mathbf b}_iDN​b^i​=ωB/N×b^i​. Consequently,

DNA=DBA+ωB/N×A.D_N\mathbf A=D_B\mathbf A+\boldsymbol\omega^{B/N}\times\mathbf A.DN​A=DB​A+ωB/N×A.

This transport theorem distinguishes an observer derivative from a basis label on components.

One transverse contribution comes from rotating the relative velocity; the other comes from increasing the carried tangential speed.

Oral checks and common error

Common error: multiplying a coordinate transformation in the wrong direction, or treating a constant component column as a constant geometric vector.

  • How do you recover BBB components from NNN components? Multiply by RNBTR_{NB}^TRNBT​; check a known basis vector.
  • Can constant speed coexist with acceleration? Yes: changing direction changes the velocity vector, as in circular motion.
  • Why does the second derivative contain two mixed terms? Each factor can be differentiated once; the product rule counts both contributions.

Continue to Generalized Coordinates for independent motion variables, or Multi-Link Transport for moving origins and angular axes. The conventions here follow the linked MIT vector notes.

Check your understanding

  • Perform vector sums, dot products and cross products in one basis.
  • Build direction-cosine matrices and distinguish re-expression from physical rotation.
  • Differentiate vector functions and products through higher orders.
