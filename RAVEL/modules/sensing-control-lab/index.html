Follow one signal around the loop

An intended human reach becomes a target position, a sensor supplies samples, an estimator approximates velocity, and a controller sends a force command to the robot. The robot moves, changing the next feedback sample. The explorer makes every link visible. The reach, sensor errors and cart motion are synthetic; no person or robot is connected.

Read the diagram. The ochre target finishes a 0.3 m reach in two seconds. The cart is the true plant state in the model. The teal cross is the delayed position sample the controller actually consumes. The plots compare those signals over the same six-second trial; their vertical cursor marks the selected time. Scene and readouts use the latest available sample, whose timestamp is shown. The red arrow indicates applied force direction; the force plot shows a held command between updates.

Begin with Nominal loop. Scrub time to compare the target and robot, then try Noisy derivative. Toggle the velocity filter while leaving noise unchanged. Finally choose Delayed loop: the controller acts on an older position and an older target. More aggressive gains can then increase oscillation and actuator clipping instead of improving tracking.

Whole-trial RMS tracking error compares true target and true cart position, including the initial reach. Force saturation counts samples where the requested magnitude exceeds 40 N. These are model diagnostics, not performance measurements from an assistive device. The model has no end stops, contact, human dynamics, anatomical inference or safety certification.

Oral practice

  • Why can filtering reduce force chatter yet hurt response? It smooths the differentiated samples but introduces phase lag.
  • Does a bounded force certify a stable loop? No. Clipping limits input magnitude; delay and feedback dynamics still govern the motion.
  • Why distinguish sensed position from true position? The controller only sees its measurement stream. Tracking error computed from that stream can conceal bias or latency.

Connect this experiment to sampled feedback, human motion sensing and impedance/admittance.

Check your understanding

  • Trace the signals from a synthetic human target to robot motion.
  • Distinguish true model state from delayed synthetic sensor samples.
  • Explain the noise and phase tradeoff of velocity smoothing.
  • Recognize force saturation and sampling assumptions.
