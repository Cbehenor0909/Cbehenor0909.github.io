Rules as consequences of the characteristic equation

For the following standard rules, use a real, proper rational L0L_0L0​ with a positive leading coefficient, absorbed into KKK, and nnn poles and mmm zeros. Count multiplicity and avoid hiding canceled internal dynamics. A negative leading coefficient changes the angle convention and the usual real-axis segments.

  • There are nnn branches, starting at open-loop poles as K→0+K\to0^+K→0+.
  • Branches approach finite zeros or, if n>mn>mn>m, extend to infinity.
  • The locus is symmetric about the real axis.
  • A real point belongs when an odd number of real poles and zeros lie to its right.

Design example: a desired point needs a zero

Suppose the desired pair is sd=−2±2js_d=-2\pm2jsd​=−2±2j. For the original loop,

K=−sd(sd+2)=4+4jK=-s_d(s_d+2)=4+4jK=−sd​(sd​+2)=4+4j

at the upper point, so scalar gain alone cannot place that pair.

Try an ideal PD compensator C(s)=K(s+z)C(s)=K(s+z)C(s)=K(s+z). The characteristic polynomial becomes

s2+(2+K)s+Kz.s^2+(2+K)s+Kz.s2+(2+K)s+Kz.

Matching (s+2−2j)(s+2+2j)=s2+4s+8(s+2-2j)(s+2+2j)=s^2+4s+8(s+2−2j)(s+2+2j)=s2+4s+8 gives K=2K=2K=2, z=4z=4z=4. The added zero changes the locus to pass through the desired poles.

However, T=2(s+4)/(s2+4s+8)T=2(s+4)/(s^2+4s+8)T=2(s+4)/(s2+4s+8) has a zero: the no-zero second-order overshoot formula no longer predicts its exact step response. The ideal derivative also amplifies high-frequency measurement noise. A physical implementation uses a filtered derivative or lead compensator and must recheck the added pole, full response and actuator limits.

Oral practice

  • Does every left-half-plane point belong to the locus? No. The angle condition restricts reachable points for a fixed loop shape and real positive gain.
  • Does larger KKK always improve settling? No. The example keeps the decay rate fixed; other loops can become unstable.
  • Can desired poles alone certify performance? No. Zeros, non-dominant modes, delays, saturation and input scaling also affect response.

Compare this geometric design with Bode loop shaping, and use stability and performance to test a chosen gain quantitatively.
