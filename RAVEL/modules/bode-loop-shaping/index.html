Bode Loop Shaping · Frequency Response, Lead and Lag

Calculate gain and phase margins from the loop transfer and design lead or lag compensation while checking crossover shifts and robustness limits.

advancedFunctionalReading lesson

How can the loop's frequency response balance tracking, disturbance rejection, noise and robustness?

A Bode plot describes one signal map

For a stable LTI system, a sinusoidal input at angular frequency ω\omegaω produces a steady-state sinusoid scaled by ∣G(jω)∣|G(j\omega)|∣G(jω)∣ and shifted by arg⁡G(jω)\arg G(j\omega)argG(jω). A Bode plot shows these quantities against logarithmic frequency:

MdB(ω)=20log⁡10∣G(jω)∣.M_{\mathrm{dB}}(\omega)=20\log_{10}|G(j\omega)|.MdB​(ω)=20log10​∣G(jω)∣.

Use ω\omegaω in rad/s\mathrm{rad/s}rad/s; ordinary frequency is f=ω/(2π)f=\omega/(2\pi)f=ω/(2π) in hertz. Closed-loop output response uses TTT, while classical stability margins use the loop transfer L=PCL=PCL=PC. Label which map is plotted.

An integrator contributes a −20 dB/decade-20\,\mathrm{dB/decade}−20dB/decade slope and −90∘-90^\circ−90∘ phase. A stable real pole 1/(1+s/ωp)1/(1+s/\omega_p)1/(1+s/ωp​) adds a high-frequency −20 dB/decade-20\,\mathrm{dB/decade}−20dB/decade and phase −arctan⁡(ω/ωp)-\arctan(\omega/\omega_p)−arctan(ω/ωp​). A left-half-plane real zero adds the opposite slope and positive phase. Right-half-plane zeros have the same magnitude contribution as their reflected zeros but different phase. A pure delay has unit magnitude and phase −ωτ-\omega\tau−ωτ. Magnitude alone does not determine robustness.

The loop-shaping chapter discusses lead and lag as performance tradeoffs. Design by specifying tracking/disturbance frequencies, noise limits and actuator constraints, then checking the complete closed loop.

Oral practice

  • Where is phase margin measured? At a unity-magnitude loop crossover, not at an arbitrary frequency or on the closed-loop TTT plot.
  • Why can a delay destabilize without changing magnitude? It adds frequency-dependent negative phase.
  • Why recheck crossover after adding lead? Lead changes magnitude as well as phase; the original crossover need not remain the relevant one.

Connect margins to sensitivity and performance and compare pole motion in root locus.

Check your understanding

  • Evaluate logarithmic magnitude and phase.
  • Compute margins at the appropriate crossover frequencies.
  • Select lead or lag parameters with a stated convention.
  • Explain why positive margins alone are not a general stability proof.
