Relative Joint Kinematics · Two Segments, One Relation

Combine calibrated segment orientations and angular velocities in a common frame, then explain the additional conventions needed for anatomical joint angles.

intermediateFunctionalReading lesson

How do two calibrated segment motions become a relative joint motion rather than a difference between unrelated angle channels?

Relative motion removes whole-system motion

If an upper arm and forearm rotate together without changing their relative pose, the elbow configuration stays the same. A world-oriented description changes, while the relative description does not. The calculation must first identify segment frames, rather than comparing the axes of two differently mounted sensors.

Read the diagram. The dashed line continues the proximal direction through the joint. The highlighted arc is the angle from that direction to the distal link. Both world angles use the same reference axis. The blue rates describe a planar model; these labels do not identify clinical elbow flexion or a validated anatomical axis.

Let B1B_1B1​ be the proximal segment frame and B2B_2B2​ the distal frame. Each orientation maps local column-vector components into the same world frame NNN. Therefore

RB1B2=RNB1TRNB2.R_{B_1B_2}=R_{NB_1}^{\mathsf T}R_{NB_2}.RB1​B2​​=RNB1​T​RNB2​​.

This maps distal coordinates into proximal coordinates. A vector that is fixed in B2B_2B2​ can now be compared with proximal axes. Apply the sensor mounting corrections from Sensor-to-Segment Calibration before using this expression.

The distal xxx axis is [0.707107,0.707107,0]T[0.707107,0.707107,0]^{\mathsf T}[0.707107,0.707107,0]T in B1B_1B1​. The angle is 45∘45^{\circ}45∘ for this declared planar convention. If both world orientations receive the same additional 30∘30^{\circ}30∘ rotation, the world angles become 50∘50^{\circ}50∘ and 95∘95^{\circ}95∘, but their relative rotation stays unchanged.

This cancellation is exact for any shared rotation QQQ applied on the left:

(QRNB1)T(QRNB2)=RNB1TRNB2.(QR_{NB_1})^{\mathsf T}(QR_{NB_2}) =R_{NB_1}^{\mathsf T}R_{NB_2}.(QRNB1​​)T(QRNB2​​)=RNB1​T​RNB2​​.

Two independent heading errors are different rotations, so they generally do not cancel. A common coordinate change and two independently drifting estimates are distinct situations.

Relative angular velocity is also a frame calculation

Angular velocities are vectors. Subtract world-resolved vectors, then express the result in B1B_1B1​:

ωB2/B1B1=RNB1T(ωB2/NN−ωB1/NN).\boldsymbol\omega_{B_2/B_1}^{B_1} =R_{NB_1}^{\mathsf T} \left(\boldsymbol\omega_{B_2/N}^{N} -\boldsymbol\omega_{B_1/N}^{N}\right).ωB2​/B1​B1​​=RNB1​T​(ωB2​/NN​−ωB1​/NN​).

If each rate is already resolved in its own segment,

ωB2/B1B1=RB1B2ωB2/NB2−ωB1/NB1.\boldsymbol\omega_{B_2/B_1}^{B_1} =R_{B_1B_2}\boldsymbol\omega_{B_2/N}^{B_2} -\boldsymbol\omega_{B_1/N}^{B_1}.ωB2​/B1​B1​​=RB1​B2​​ωB2​/NB2​​−ωB1​/NB1​​.

Rates measured in sensor coordinates first require the corresponding RBiSiR_{B_iS_i}RBi​Si​​ transform. In the planar example, rates of 10∘/s10^{\circ}/\mathrm{s}10∘/s and 35∘/s35^{\circ}/\mathrm{s}35∘/s both lie along the same positive zzz axis. The relative rate is therefore 25∘/s25^{\circ}/\mathrm{s}25∘/s, or 0.436332 rad/s0.436332\,\mathrm{rad/s}0.436332rad/s. For spatial motion, subtracting untransformed three-channel arrays gives the wrong vector.

A rotation matrix is not yet three anatomical angles

To report anatomical angles, define segment axes from a stated calibration and choose a joint coordinate system or rotation sequence, sign convention and neutral configuration. ISB’s joint-specific recommendations address reporting conventions for the shoulder, elbow, wrist and hand. A generic sensor yaw–pitch–roll sequence does not establish those conventions.

Subtracting three Euler-angle triplets generally does not produce the Euler angles of the relative rotation: finite spatial rotations do not commute. An Euler sequence also has singular configurations; a quaternion avoids that representation singularity but does not remove an ambiguity in anatomical axes. VectorNav’s attitude transformations distinguish Euler parameters and angular-rate kinematics.

The model assumes rigid segment attachments, compatible world frames, synchronized timestamps and sufficiently accurate orientation estimates. Human soft-tissue motion and attachment slip can violate that model. Relative orientation alone also supplies no joint-center position or translational displacement. Two IMUs consequently do not guarantee an anatomical joint angle.

Separate fit residuals from measurement accuracy

Poitras et al. (2019) systematically review joint-angle validity and reliability across joints and tasks. Check which joint, movement, reference method and error metric support a result. A repeatable estimate can still have systematic error; findings for one joint/task do not establish a universal IMU accuracy.

Paper figure — Al Borno et al. (2022), Figure 4. Reproduced unmodified from OpenSense, © 2022 the authors, CC BY 4.0. Select the image for full resolution; source axes and notation are preserved.

Read the paper figure. Both axes show changes from minute 1 to minute 10. The vertical quantity is the IK fit mismatch between estimated sensor orientation and the fitted model’s virtual-IMU orientation. The horizontal quantity uses an independent optical orientation reference on the same sensor plate. Their association makes residuals informative in this study; a small residual alone does not certify anatomical joint-angle accuracy. The paper’s lower-extremity model and evaluation bound that inference.

See the paper guide for related methods and reviews.

Common errors

  • Subtract Euler channels instead of composing calibrated rotations.
  • Subtract vectors resolved in different sensor frames.
  • Assume that separate magnetic heading errors cancel.
  • Compare asynchronous poses during fast movement.

Oral checks

  1. Why does a shared world rotation cancel from the relative matrix?
  2. Which transform precedes subtraction of distal and proximal rates?
  3. What extra definitions turn a relative rotation into a reported anatomical angle?

Connect the result to Human Motion Sensing and the moving-axis calculations in Multi-Link Transport.

Check your understanding

  • Compute distal orientation expressed in a proximal segment frame.
  • Subtract angular velocities only after expressing them in the same frame.
  • State when common world-heading errors cancel.
  • Separate relative orientation from an anatomical joint-angle convention.
