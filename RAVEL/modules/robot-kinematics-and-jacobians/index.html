Robot Kinematics and Jacobians

Map joint motion into tool motion, map contact forces back into joint torques, and recognize the task directions lost at a singularity.

intermediateFunctionalReading lesson

How does moving a joint move the tool, and how does pushing the tool load the joints?

First ask which point and frame

A human hand estimate and a robot tool target become comparable only after their coordinate frames, units and reference points are defined. Forward kinematics starts with joint coordinates and produces a tool pose. Inverse kinematics asks which joint coordinates could realize a desired pose. Neither problem determines the forces needed to move the robot.

Read the diagram. The tool is at the end of the second link. Blue describes its instantaneous velocity; red describes an applied task force. One Jacobian connects both arrows to the joints, through two different maps. The geometry is schematic; the worked lengths and angles below define the numerical example.

For a rigid tool pose relative to world frame WWW,

TWT(q)=[RWT(q)pWT(q)0T1].T_{WT}(\mathbf q)=\begin{bmatrix}R_{WT}(\mathbf q)&\mathbf p_{WT}(\mathbf q)\\\mathbf0^T&1\end{bmatrix}.TWT​(q)=[RWT​(q)0T​pWT​(q)1​].

Here RRR specifies orientation and p\mathbf pp specifies the tool-origin position. For that point’s translational task,

vT=Jp(q)q˙,aT=Jpq¨+J˙pq˙.\mathbf v_T=J_p(\mathbf q)\dot{\mathbf q},\qquad \mathbf a_T=J_p\ddot{\mathbf q}+\dot J_p\dot{\mathbf q}.vT​=Jp​(q)q˙​,aT​=Jp​q¨​+J˙p​q˙​.

A geometric six-component Jacobian instead maps joint rates to angular velocity and tool-point velocity. A body twist expresses rigid motion in the tool frame; a spatial twist expresses it in the world frame, whose linear component is generally not the tool-point velocity. Declare the convention before mixing six-component quantities. The Modern Robotics Jacobian lesson develops the twist convention.

For a task force F=[4,3]T N\mathbf F=[4,3]^T\,\mathrm NF=[4,3]TN, virtual power gives

τtask=JpTF=[−2.8+0.93−1.6] N m,τtaskTq˙=FTvT≈−0.0882 W.\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol\tau_{\mathrm{task}}&=J_p^T\mathbf F =\begin{bmatrix}-2.8+0.9\sqrt3\\-1.6\end{bmatrix}\,\mathrm{N\,m},\\ \boldsymbol\tau_{\mathrm{task}}^T\dot{\mathbf q} &=\mathbf F^T\mathbf v_T\approx-0.0882\,\mathrm W. \end{aligned}τtask​τtaskT​q˙​​=JpT​F=[−2.8+0.93​−1.6​]Nm,=FTvT​≈−0.0882W.​

This equality is an independent sign and unit check. If F\mathbf FF is a force on the robot, these are external generalized torques. To balance it statically, actuator torques include their negative, together with gravity and other loads. For a wrench, pair moment and force with the corresponding angular and linear velocities in the same frame and at the same reference point. See Statics of Open Chains.

A singularity removes a task direction

Read the diagram. At the straight configuration, either revolute joint initially moves the tool perpendicular to the links. Their velocities cannot create a first-order motion along the extended arm. The joints still move; the point task loses one independent direction.

For this two-link point task, det⁡Jp=l1l2sin⁡q2\det J_p=l_1l_2\sin q_2detJp​=l1​l2​sinq2​. Close to q2=0q_2=0q2​=0, demanding motion in a weak direction can require very large joint rates. A regularized local solve is

q˙=JpT(JpJpT+λ2I)−1vd,λ>0.\dot{\mathbf q}=J_p^T(J_pJ_p^T+\lambda^2I)^{-1}\mathbf v_d, \qquad \lambda>0.q˙​=JpT​(Jp​JpT​+λ2I)−1vd​,λ>0.

It minimizes ∥Jpq˙−vd∥2+λ2∥q˙∥2\|J_p\dot{\mathbf q}-\mathbf v_d\|^2+\lambda^2\|\dot{\mathbf q}\|^2∥Jp​q˙​−vd​∥2+λ2∥q˙​∥2: smaller rate amplification costs task error. For revolute joints and a position task, scale λ\lambdaλ consistently with the Jacobian’s length units. Mixed translation, rotation and prismatic tasks require deliberate weighting. Damping cannot create an unavailable direction, enforce collision or joint limits, or guarantee convergence of a finite nonlinear move. Buss’s derivation explains this local tradeoff.

Oral checks and common errors

  • Why is force mapped by the transpose? The joint and task descriptions must give the same instantaneous mechanical power.
  • Does a reachable pose guarantee a well-conditioned velocity map? No. Reachability and instantaneous rank are different questions.
  • What changes if the tool contact point moves? Its translational Jacobian and the wrench moment reference change.

Common errors: using J−1J^{-1}J−1 to map forces, mixing body and world frames, or treating a damped inverse as a complete motion planner. Continue with Robot Dynamics and Actuation to calculate the actuator effort behind the motion.

Check your understanding

  • Distinguish a forward pose map from its instantaneous velocity map.
  • Apply the transpose Jacobian with a consistent frame, point and force convention.
  • Calculate a planar example and diagnose a singular task direction.
  • Explain the tradeoff introduced by damped least squares.
