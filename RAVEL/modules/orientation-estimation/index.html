Orientation Estimation · Rates, Gravity and Heading

Integrate gyroscope rates with explicit rotation conventions, understand accelerometer specific force, and explain why fusion needs observable reference directions.

intermediateFunctionalReading lesson

How can noisy rates and reference directions constrain orientation without becoming direct joint-angle measurements?

Prediction and correction answer different questions

A gyroscope tells us how orientation changes over a short interval. A reference direction helps constrain accumulated error. Fusion combines these roles under stated assumptions; it does not turn every acceleration vector into gravity or every magnetic field into an undisturbed compass.

Read the diagram. Blue boxes represent model prediction and an orientation estimate. Teal inputs are symbolic measurements, not recorded data. Gravity constrains tilt; the optional magnetic reference constrains heading only when its environmental assumptions hold. The return arrow carries the updated estimate into the next time step.

Why acceleration is an imperfect reference

An ideal accelerometer reads specific force,

fS=RNST(aN−gN).\mathbf f_S=R_{NS}^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf a_N-\mathbf g_N).fS​=RNST​(aN​−gN​).

For a supported stationary sensor with gN=[0,0,−9.81]T m/s2\mathbf g_N=[0,0,-9.81]^{\mathsf T}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}gN​=[0,0,−9.81]Tm/s2, this becomes RNST[0,0,9.81]TR_{NS}^{\mathsf T}[0,0,9.81]^{\mathsf T}RNST​[0,0,9.81]T. If translation is negligible, −fS/∥fS∥-\mathbf f_S/\|\mathbf f_S\|−fS​/∥fS​∥ estimates the downward gravity direction in SSS. During free fall, the ideal reading is zero and this normalization supplies no direction. Sustained translation or rotational point acceleration can imitate tilt.

Gravity alone leaves rotation about the vertical unobservable. Gyro propagation can carry heading forward, but cannot correct its unknown initial value or bias drift without additional information. A magnetometer provides another direction, yet nearby motors, metal and changing fields can violate the reference model. VectorNav’s AHRS discussion describes these sensor contributions and limitations.

Complementarity before a full filter

θ^k=λ(θ^k−1+ωkΔt)+(1−λ)θa,k,0≤λ≤1.\hat\theta_k=\lambda\bigl(\hat\theta_{k-1}+\omega_k\Delta t\bigr) +(1-\lambda)\theta_{a,k},\qquad 0\leq\lambda\leq1.θ^k​=λ(θ^k−1​+ωk​Δt)+(1−λ)θa,k​,0≤λ≤1.

A published quaternion-filter architecture

Paper figure — Valenti, Dryanovski and Xiao (2015), Figure 2. Reproduced unmodified from Keeping a Good Attitude, © 2015 the authors, CC BY 4.0. Select the image for full resolution; source notation is preserved.

Read the paper figure. Gyroscope propagation predicts orientation; the accelerometer supplies correction after transformation and interpolation. This IMU-only diagram does not show the paper’s additional magnetic correction for MARG sensors. The paper writes a global-to-local inverse quaternion, while this lesson uses sensor-to-world orientation; reverse the transform direction before comparing propagation signs. It is a quaternion algorithm, rather than the scalar tilt update above. Dynamic acceleration is not pure gravity, and gravity alone supplies no heading reference.

The paper guide pairs this method with calibration and joint-kinematics evidence.

Common errors

  • Integrate degrees as radians or mix scalar-first and scalar-last quaternions.
  • Equate three gyro channels with three Euler-angle derivatives.
  • Trust gravity correction during arbitrary acceleration, or trust magnetic heading near a disturbance.
  • Call a sensor orientation an anatomical joint angle before mounting calibration.

Oral checks

  1. What information supplies the initial orientation?
  2. Why does a stationary accelerometer read approximately 1g1g1g?
  3. Which reference is missing when yaw drifts despite a stable tilt estimate?

Check your understanding

  • Relate body-resolved gyroscope rates to a rotation-matrix derivative.
  • State the quaternion order and multiplication convention.
  • Explain specific force and the gravity-only approximation.
  • Identify heading ambiguity and limits of magnetic corrections.
