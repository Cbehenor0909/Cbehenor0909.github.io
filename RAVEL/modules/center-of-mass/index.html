Start with three experiments

  1. Two masses: m1=1 kgm_1 = 1\,\mathrm{kg}m1​=1kg and m2=3 kgm_2 = 3\,\mathrm{kg}m2​=3kg, separated by 1.2 m1.2\,\mathrm{m}1.2m. Predict which mass the COM is closer to, then vary m2m_2m2​. Scaling both masses equally would leave COM position unchanged.
  2. Rod and annulus: A uniform rod’s COM is at its midpoint. Change its density gradient: COM shifts even though the geometry stays the same. An annulus has its COM in its empty central hole; COM need not be a material point.
  3. Human segments: Select a segment and change its mass or local COM fraction. Enable motion and inspect velocity/acceleration. A fixed local COM can still move in Ground because its segment rotates.

The 12-body human schematic is a teaching construction, not an OpenSim or subject-specific model. Default masses total 70 kg70\,\mathrm{kg}70kg, with every local COM fraction f=0.5f = 0.5f=0.5. These values are not anatomical standards. Masses remain constant during each prescribed trajectory.

Point centroid, geometric centroid and mass center

An unweighted centroid of nnn selected points is rpoints=1n∑iri\mathbf r_{\mathrm{points}}=\frac1n\sum_i\mathbf r_irpoints​=n1​∑i​ri​. It treats every selected point equally, so adding more markers to one region can move it even when the physical object has not changed. It is not a mass estimate unless equal point weights represent equal masses.

A geometric centroid uses the relevant geometric measure: cA=1A∫r dA\mathbf c_A=\frac1A\int\mathbf r\,dAcA​=A1​∫rdA for an area, or cV=1V∫r dV\mathbf c_V=\frac1V\int\mathbf r\,dVcV​=V1​∫rdV for a volume. COM instead weights mass. These coincide for a uniform-density plate or solid respectively, but a nonuniform object generally has distinct geometric and mass centers. A polygon’s area centroid is also generally different from the arithmetic mean of its vertices.

For the two-mass example, the unweighted point midpoint is 0 m0\,\mathrm m0m, while COM is 0.3 m0.3\,\mathrm m0.3m. The displayed rod retains the same geometric centroid L/2L/2L/2 when its density gradient changes, yet its COM shifts. These are useful qualifying-exam checks: state what is being averaged and why its weights describe the physical question.

Read the diagram. The ochre point is the intersection of the medians at (1,1) m(1,1)\,\mathrm m(1,1)m. A thin vertical strip contributes its whole area, whose height is 3−x3-x3−x, rather than only two selected points. Uniform area density makes this area centroid the COM. Equal masses at the vertices happen to give the same center for this triangle, but their inertia differs.

As a geometric example, a uniform triangular plate has vertices (0,0)(0,0)(0,0), (3,0)(3,0)(3,0) and (0,3)(0,3)(0,3) in meters. Its area is A=4.5 m2A=4.5\,\mathrm{m^2}A=4.5m2. For 0≤x≤30\leq x\leq30≤x≤3, the local height is 3−x3-x3−x, giving

xA=14.5∫03x(3−x) dx=1 m,yA=1 m.x_A=\frac1{4.5}\int_0^3 x(3-x)\,dx=1\,\mathrm m, \qquad y_A=1\,\mathrm m.xA​=4.51​∫03​x(3−x)dx=1m,yA​=1m.

Thus its area centroid is (1,1) m(1,1)\,\mathrm m(1,1)m, also the vertex average in this special triangular case. Uniform area density makes this its COM. Equal point masses at the three vertices would have the same COM location but a different mass distribution and inertia.

Human segments: local COM is not a joint center

For segment iii, define mass mim_imi​, local COM vector ci\mathbf{c}_ici​, frame-origin position rOi\mathbf{r}_{O_i}rOi​​ and rotation RiR_iRi​ from the body frame into Ground:

ri=rOi+Rici,rC=1M∑imiri.\mathbf{r}_i = \mathbf{r}_{O_i} + R_i\mathbf{c}_i, \qquad \mathbf{r}_C = \frac{1}{M}\sum_i m_i\mathbf{r}_i.ri​=rOi​​+Ri​ci​,rC​=M1​i∑​mi​ri​.

A joint center, marker, body-frame origin and segment COM are different points. Averaging joint centers is not a whole-body COM calculation. A fraction fff from proximal to distal gives ci=fLie^\mathbf{c}_i = fL_i\hat{\mathbf{e}}ci​=fLi​e^ only for an on-axis approximation. Specify endpoints and the local axis; real COM offsets may have three nonzero components.

The schematic lumps trunk/pelvis, head/neck, upper arms, forearms/hands, thighs, shanks and feet into 12 bodies, including both sides. Arms attach to moving trunk points; legs attach to the translating pelvis. Directions are prescribed in a plane, with horizontal xxx, vertical yyy and out-of-plane zzz. There are no contact constraints, muscle forces or realistic gait assumptions.

Regressions such as de Leva (1996) provide segment parameters for defined populations and conventions, rather than universal individual measurements. Keep mass fractions, segment boundaries, length definitions and COM reference endpoints consistent. For OpenSim, inspect the actual scaled model instead of substituting this page’s teaching values.

From position to velocity and acceleration

For constant masses in one fixed inertial frame:

vC=1M∑imivi,aC=1M∑imiai.\mathbf{v}_C = \frac{1}{M}\sum_i m_i\mathbf{v}_i, \qquad \mathbf{a}_C = \frac{1}{M}\sum_i m_i\mathbf{a}_i.vC​=M1​i∑​mi​vi​,aC​=M1​i∑​mi​ai​.

Differentiate vector position, not its magnitude. Differentiating local-basis components also requires the rotating-basis contribution. Time-varying masses require additional terms and are outside this lesson.

Let ρi=Rici\boldsymbol{\rho}_i = R_i\mathbf{c}_iρi​=Ri​ci​ be a fixed local COM offset expressed in Ground. Then

vi=vOi+ωi×ρi,ai=aOi+αi×ρi+ωi×(ωi×ρi).\begin{aligned} \mathbf{v}_i &= \mathbf{v}_{O_i} + \boldsymbol{\omega}_i \times \boldsymbol{\rho}_i, \\ \mathbf{a}_i &= \mathbf{a}_{O_i} + \boldsymbol{\alpha}_i \times \boldsymbol{\rho}_i \\ &\quad + \boldsymbol{\omega}_i \times (\boldsymbol{\omega}_i \times \boldsymbol{\rho}_i). \end{aligned}vi​ai​​=vOi​​+ωi​×ρi​,=aOi​​+αi​×ρi​+ωi​×(ωi​×ρi​).​

This is the rigid-body special case of the five-term transport formula: relative velocity/acceleration vanish, so a fixed segment COM has no Coriolis term. For moving origins and angular axes, see Multi-Link Transport.

Sampling and derivative quality

In “Position → velocity → acceleration,” the solid curves are synthetic analytic truth. Dashed curves use sampled positions and local quadratic differentiation, with no filtering. Optional perturbation is a deterministic 8 Hz sine, not measured noise.

For uniform Δt\Delta tΔt, interior estimates are

vi≈ri+1−ri−12Δt,ai≈ri+1−2ri+ri−1Δt2.\begin{aligned} \mathbf{v}_i &\approx \frac{\mathbf{r}_{i+1} - \mathbf{r}_{i-1}}{2\Delta t}, \\ \mathbf{a}_i &\approx \frac{\mathbf{r}_{i+1} - 2\mathbf{r}_i + \mathbf{r}_{i-1}}{\Delta t^2}. \end{aligned}vi​ai​​≈2Δtri+1​−ri−1​​,≈Δt2ri+1​−2ri​+ri−1​​.​

The implementation also supports unequal timestamps and omits endpoint derivatives.

Differentiation amplifies high-frequency errors. For recordings, preserve original samples; check timestamps, units and gaps; document interpolation, filtering, cutoff selection and edges. Differentiate a consistent fitted trajectory. Filtering coordinates before forward kinematics and filtering COM afterward are not generally equivalent because rotations are nonlinear; inspect processing sensitivity.

OpenSim calculations

An OpenSim Body defines mass in kg and a local COM vector in its Body frame, as documented by the Body API. A mesh or body-frame origin does not define COM.

At a compatible State, the Model API provides whole-model queries:

Quantity Realization stage Whole model Per-body station
Position Position calcMassCenterPosition(state) findStationLocationInGround(state, body.getMassCenter())
Velocity Velocity calcMassCenterVelocity(state) findStationVelocityInGround(state, body.getMassCenter())
Acceleration Acceleration calcMassCenterAcceleration(state) findStationAccelerationInGround(state, body.getMassCenter())

The Frame API defines station results relative to and expressed in Ground. “In Ground” does not mean stationary. Common OpenSim models use y vertical, but verify your model’s Ground/gravity and angle conventions.

BodyKinematics records per-body COM kinematics and whole-model COM kinematics. This is a GUI/Analyze Tool route when its input and derivative processing match your intended calculation.

Choose the acceleration you mean

Kinematics-derived: Use the actual scaled model and a complete, consistent motion trajectory. Document fitting/filtering, constraints, q\mathbf{q}q, generalized speeds u\mathbf{u}u and their derivatives. An imposed-motion route can use the installed version’s PositionMotion coordinate functions. Supply all required coordinates, verify units/constraints, and evaluate the prescribed motion before querying derivatives.

Dynamics-derived: A forward-dynamics State with compatible forces, controls and constraints supplies acceleration from that dynamical model. It is not automatically the acceleration of a measured trajectory.

Position-only IK is insufficient. Setting coordinate values, leaving speeds at defaults and calling realizeAcceleration() does not generate IK-derived acceleration. Generalized speed u\mathbf{u}u is not universally equal to q˙\dot{\mathbf{q}}q˙​. Realization enables access to quantities; it does not replace consistent inputs.

If only a reliable Ground COM position series is available, numerical differentiation is a separate analysis path. Label results as sample-derived and record processing provenance.

Analysis checks

  • Model: Record version, scaling, body coverage, masses and local COMs. Added equipment and model modifications matter; a partial model yields subsystem COM.
  • Frames: Use one Ground frame and verify kg, m, s, angular units and the vertical axis.
  • Trajectory: Ensure position, speed and acceleration describe the same constrained motion. Preserve originals and keep derived outputs separate.
  • Reconciliation: Compare mass-weighted per-body Ground r\mathbf{r}r, v\mathbf{v}v and a\mathbf{a}a with whole-model queries at the same State on a standard body-based model. Report discrepancies.
  • Derivatives: Inspect static intervals, gaps, edges and smoothing sensitivity. Compare model derivatives with finite differences on smooth interior intervals.
  • Mechanics: For constant masses in an inertial frame, MaC=∑FexternalM\mathbf{a}_C = \sum\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{external}}MaC​=∑Fexternal​, including gravity. Internal action/reaction pairs cancel; COM geometry alone does not supply forces.

COM, COP and balance

COM coincides with center of gravity in an approximately uniform gravity field. Center of pressure describes contact loading and is a different quantity. A COM projection within the base of support does not establish dynamic stability by itself. Velocity, external forces and changing support matter. XCoM and margin-of-stability models require additional assumptions and remain outside this lesson.

Oral practice

“I define each segment’s mass and local center of mass, transform the centers into Ground, and form a mass-weighted average. For constant masses, the same weights apply to segment velocities and accelerations. Segment rotation contributes even when the local COM is fixed. In OpenSim, I check whether the State represents prescribed kinematics or forward dynamics before interpreting acceleration.”

Check your understanding

  • Compute COM for point masses and continuous distributions.
  • Transform local segment COMs into Ground before combining bodies.
  • Calculate COM velocity and acceleration for constant masses.
  • Distinguish kinematic derivatives from dynamics-derived OpenSim acceleration.
  • Explain why COM position alone does not establish dynamic balance.
