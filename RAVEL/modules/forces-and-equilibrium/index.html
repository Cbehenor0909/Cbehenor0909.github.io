Forces, Moments and Equilibrium

Build a free-body diagram, reduce a force system to a force and couple, and check equilibrium against the actual contact constraints.

intermediateFunctionalReading lesson

What force and moment does a load system produce, and can the available supports balance both?

Draw the system before writing equations

A free-body diagram chooses a body or collection of bodies, cuts its connections to the environment, and shows the loads acting on that chosen system. Include gravity, applied forces, contact reactions and applied couples. Place every force at its application point and declare the positive directions.

An interaction between two selected bodies is internal to their combined system, but external to either isolated body. Action and reaction belong on different free-body diagrams. A pin can transmit force components while an ideal frictionless pin supplies no resisting moment about its free rotational axis. A support model is a physical assumption, not a symbol chosen to make the algebra solvable.

A force has a moment about a point

For force F\mathbf FF applied at PPP, its moment about OOO is

τO=rOP×F.\boldsymbol\tau_O=\mathbf r_{OP}\times\mathbf F.τO​=rOP​×F.

The lever arm is the perpendicular distance to the force’s line of action. Changing OOO changes this moment. Moving a force along its own line of action preserves a rigid body’s resultant force and moment; moving it to an arbitrary point requires an added couple.

A couple consists of equal and opposite forces on distinct parallel lines. Its resultant force is zero, but its moment d×F\mathbf d\times\mathbf Fd×F need not be zero. This moment is independent of the reference point. A pure torque therefore cannot be replaced by one force without changing the net force.

For many applied forces and free couples, the equivalent system at OOO is

R=∑iFi,MO=∑irOPi×Fi+∑jCj.\mathbf R=\sum_i\mathbf F_i,\qquad \mathbf M_O=\sum_i\mathbf r_{OP_i}\times\mathbf F_i+\sum_j\mathbf C_j.R=i∑​Fi​,MO​=i∑​rOPi​​×Fi​+j∑​Cj​.

The replacement preserves the external rigid-body equations. It does not preserve the original distribution of internal stress. On changing the reference point to QQQ, MQ=MO−rOQ×R\mathbf M_Q=\mathbf M_O-\mathbf r_{OQ}\times\mathbf RMQ​=MO​−rOQ​×R. In 3-D, a general resultant wrench need not be reducible to a single force alone.

Gravity and contact

In a uniform gravitational field, the distributed gravitational loading reduces to MgM\mathbf gMg acting at the COM because ∫r×g dm=rC×Mg\int\mathbf r\times\mathbf g\,dm=\mathbf r_C\times M\mathbf g∫r×gdm=rC​×Mg. This is why COM and center of gravity coincide under that assumption. A spatially varying gravity field requires integrating the actual loading.

Contact forces depend on the contact model. A nonadhesive support can push but cannot pull. For a sticking Coulomb contact,

Fn≥0,∥Ft∥≤μsFn.F_n\geq0,\qquad \lVert\mathbf F_t\rVert\leq\mu_sF_n.Fn​≥0,∥Ft​∥≤μs​Fn​.

Static friction adjusts within the bound; it is not always μsFn\mu_sF_nμs​Fn​. At sliding, a kinetic-friction model supplies a force opposing relative slip. Negative normal reactions or violated friction bounds reject the assumed contact mode and require a new free-body diagram.

Worked beam equilibrium

Take a horizontal rigid beam with supports at AAA, x=0x=0x=0, and BBB, x=2 mx=2\,\mathrm mx=2m. A pin at AAA supplies horizontal and vertical reactions; a roller at BBB supplies an upward normal reaction. A 100 N100\,\mathrm N100N load acts downward at x=1.5 mx=1.5\,\mathrm mx=1.5m, and the beam’s own 40 N40\,\mathrm N40N weight acts at x=1 mx=1\,\mathrm mx=1m.

Read the diagram. Choose the beam as the system. The red arrows are forces on the beam, and the bottom ruler gives their moment arms about AAA. The force at BBB has a 2 m2\,\mathrm m2m lever arm; the two downward loads have 1 m1\,\mathrm m1m and 1.5 m1.5\,\mathrm m1.5m arms. The displayed reactions are the values solved below. Arrow lengths are schematic, not proportional to force magnitudes.

For static equilibrium, resultant force and moment vanish. Moments about AAA eliminate its reactions:

2By−100(1.5)−40(1)=0,By=95 N,Ay+By−100−40=0,Ay=45 N,Ax=0.\begin{aligned} 2B_y-100(1.5)-40(1)&=0,\\ B_y&=95\,\mathrm N,\\ A_y+B_y-100-40&=0,\\ A_y&=45\,\mathrm N,\qquad A_x=0. \end{aligned}2By​−100(1.5)−40(1)By​Ay​+By​−100−40Ay​​=0,=95N,=0,=45N,Ax​=0.​

Both vertical reactions are nonnegative, so the assumed supporting contacts are feasible for this loading. A moment check about BBB independently gives −2Ay+100(0.5)+40(1)=0-2A_y+100(0.5)+40(1)=0−2Ay​+100(0.5)+40(1)=0. Notice that the combined downward force is 140 N140\,\mathrm N140N, applied at x=190/140≈1.357 mx=190/140\approx1.357\,\mathrm mx=190/140≈1.357m if reduced to one force. The load-weighted location is essential; using the geometric midpoint changes the reactions.

For a spatial rigid body, static equilibrium gives three force equations and three moment equations. Additional unknowns may require deformation or contact information; equilibrium alone does not resolve every statically indeterminate system.

Oral checks and common error

Common error: balancing forces while forgetting moments, or treating an assumed contact reaction as automatically physically admissible.

  • Can zero resultant force still rotate a body? Yes. A nonzero couple can change angular momentum while the COM has zero acceleration.
  • Why choose moments about a support? Forces passing through that point have zero lever arm and drop out, simplifying the equations.
  • Does COM inside a base of support prove dynamic balance? No. That geometric condition omits velocity and the forces required for acceleration; see Base of Support.

For joint-level statics and force transmission, see the MIT statics chapter. Continue to Newton–Euler Dynamics when acceleration is nonzero.

Check your understanding

  • Separate external loads from internal interactions on a chosen system.
  • Replace distributed loads by an equivalent resultant force and moment.
  • Solve static equilibrium and verify unilateral contact and friction conditions.
