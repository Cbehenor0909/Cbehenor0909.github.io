Signal Processing and State Estimation · Noise, Timing and Confidence

Follow sampling, filtering, differentiation and model-based estimation, with explicit noise assumptions and a computed scalar Kalman update.

How do we turn sampled measurements into useful feedback while retaining uncertainty and timing information?

A useful feedback signal needs a time and a meaning

A smooth trace can still be delayed, biased or expressed in the wrong frame. Start by defining the signal’s units, reference frame, acquisition timestamp, sampling interval and provenance. Processing changes the relationship between the quantity at acquisition and the estimate available to a controller.

Read the diagram. Teal marks symbolic measurements; blue marks model calculations. The anti-alias stage precedes sampling. The estimator uses a known input as well as measurements, and the controller receives timing and confidence alongside the estimated state. The drawing is a conceptual pipeline, not a live acquisition system.

Sampling can hide a different motion

For a band-limited signal, the sampling frequency must exceed twice its highest retained frequency. Real anti-alias filters need a transition band and sufficient attenuation before acquisition, or before downsampling an already sampled signal. Analog Devices MT-002 explains this design distinction.

At fs=100 Hzf_s=100\,\mathrm{Hz}fs​=100Hz, a model cosine at 70 Hz70\,\mathrm{Hz}70Hz produces exactly the same samples as a cosine at 30 Hz30\,\mathrm{Hz}30Hz:

cos⁡ ⁣(2π70k100)=cos⁡ ⁣(2π30k100),k∈Z.\cos\!\left(2\pi\frac{70k}{100}\right) =\cos\!\left(2\pi\frac{30k}{100}\right),\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.cos(2π10070k​)=cos(2π10030k​),k∈Z.

Filtering those samples afterward cannot identify which analog signal generated them. A nominal sample rate also does not establish synchronized acquisition across sensors; use actual timestamps and an explicit alignment model.

Worked confidence-weighted update

Use a synthetic prior x^−=0.5 rad\hat x^-=0.5\,\mathrm{rad}x^−=0.5rad, P−=0.04 rad2P^-=0.04\,\mathrm{rad^2}P−=0.04rad2 and symbolic measurement z=0.7 radz=0.7\,\mathrm{rad}z=0.7rad with R=0.01 rad2R=0.01\,\mathrm{rad^2}R=0.01rad2. Then K=0.8K=0.8K=0.8, ν=0.2 rad\nu=0.2\,\mathrm{rad}ν=0.2rad, x^+=0.66 rad\hat x^+=0.66\,\mathrm{rad}x^+=0.66rad and P+=0.008 rad2P^+=0.008\,\mathrm{rad^2}P+=0.008rad2. Gaussian assumptions give the usual conditional-mean interpretation; correct linear models and second moments also support minimum-variance linear estimation. This scalar model does not represent quaternion dynamics. Its covariance omits unmodeled mounting errors and systematic bias. Persistent innovations can reveal mismatch; changing Q,RQ,RQ,R cannot repair a wrong frame, attachment or clock.

Read an estimator as a complete method

Valenti, Dryanovski and Xiao (2015) give a quaternion-based orientation-filter method with prediction and reference correction. Its original architecture figure helps distinguish those roles. The scalar Kalman calculation here is a separate teaching model, rather than an implementation of that filter. For either method, identify the state, noise/reference assumptions, update timing and validation reference before comparing results.

Common errors

  • Treat smoothing as an anti-alias correction after acquisition.
  • Differentiate without checking interval, noise and phase delay.
  • Interpret covariance as a measured accuracy guarantee.
  • Tune away a persistent calibration or timing error.

Oral checks

  1. Why can the 70 Hz70\,\mathrm{Hz}70Hz example masquerade as 30 Hz30\,\mathrm{Hz}30Hz?
  2. What happens to difference noise when Δt\Delta tΔt halves?
  3. Why does a small innovation not prove anatomical accuracy?

Inspect the synthetic Sensing–Control Lab to relate an estimate’s timing to the controller using it.

Check your understanding

  • Explain aliasing and the order of filtering and sampling.
  • Calculate derivative noise under independent-sample assumptions.
  • Distinguish a low-pass smoother from a model-based observer.
  • Compute a scalar Kalman update and interpret its covariance.
