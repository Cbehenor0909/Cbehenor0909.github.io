Three views of the same dynamics

Position alone does not specify how a moving system will evolve. A mass at x=1x=1x=1 can be moving toward the origin, moving away, or momentarily at rest. The phase plane retains both position xxx and velocity vvv in one point, z=[x,v]T\mathbf{z}=[x,v]^\mathsf{T}z=[x,v]T.

The arrows have equal display length: they indicate direction, while the numeric derivative readout supplies magnitude. The selected initial state determines one trajectory through that field. The distinction between a state-space field and an individual motion is also developed in MIT’s pendulum notes. Here the simpler linear model lets us calculate the motion exactly.

Declare the model and its scale

The explorer uses an unforced, autonomous, normalized second-order system:

x˙=v,v˙=−kx−cv,z˙=Az,A=[01−k−c].\begin{aligned} \dot x&=v,\\ \dot v&=-kx-cv,\\ \dot{\mathbf z}&=A\mathbf z, & A&=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-k&-c\end{bmatrix}. \end{aligned}x˙v˙z˙​=v,=−kx−cv,=Az,​A​=[0−k​1−c​].​

All displayed variables are dimensionless, including time. The controls cover −2≤k≤4-2\leq k\leq4−2≤k≤4, −2≤c≤4-2\leq c\leq4−2≤c≤4, initial coordinates between −2-2−2 and 222, and 0≤t≤60\leq t\leq60≤t≤6. The parameter kkk is a restoring coefficient and ccc is a damping coefficient. Positive kkk restores position; positive ccc opposes velocity. Negative values deliberately expose unstable dynamics. Negative damping represents energy supplied to the model, rather than ordinary passive friction.

To connect this normalization to mq¨+Cq˙+Kq=0m\ddot q+C\dot q+Kq=0mq¨​+Cq˙​+Kq=0, choose a length scale LLL and time scale TTT. With x=q/Lx=q/Lx=q/L and dimensionless t=tphysical/Tt=t_{\mathrm{physical}}/Tt=tphysical​/T,

Thus a displayed c=3c=3c=3 is not a damping coefficient of 3 N s/m3\,\mathrm{N\,s/m}3Ns/m without specifying the physical scales.

The phase window is fixed at −3≤x,v≤3-3\leq x,v\leq3−3≤x,v≤3. A curve leaving the window continues evolving; the edge is a viewing limit, not a wall, saturation or stop condition. The time traces share an automatically scaled vertical axis. Compare their tick labels when changing parameters: a visually similar curve can represent a much larger state. Axis rescaling can stretch a phase orbit, but does not change its eigenvalues or stability.

Read the field before reading eigenvalues

For any k,ck,ck,c, v>0v>0v>0 means motion to the right and v<0v<0v<0 means motion to the left. On the horizontal axis v=0v=0v=0, the field is vertical because x˙=0\dot x=0x˙=0. If k>0k>0k>0, a state at (1,0)(1,0)(1,0) initially points downward: its position is momentarily stationary while its velocity begins becoming negative.

The other nullcline, kx+cv=0kx+cv=0kx+cv=0, has v˙=0\dot v=0v˙=0 and horizontal flow, except where both derivatives vanish. A nullcline makes one derivative zero; it is not generally a trajectory or a set of equilibria. An equilibrium requires both derivatives to vanish. When k≠0k\ne0k=0, the only equilibrium is (0,0)(0,0)(0,0).

The characteristic equation and eigenvalues follow directly from the matrix:

det⁡(λI−A)=λ2+cλ+k=0,λ1,2=−c±c2−4k2.\det(\lambda I-A)=\lambda^2+c\lambda+k=0, \qquad \lambda_{1,2}=\frac{-c\pm\sqrt{c^2-4k}}{2}.det(λI−A)=λ2+cλ+k=0,λ1,2​=2−c±c2−4k​​.

With k>0,c>0k>0,c>0k>0,c>0, every initial state converges to the origin: the linear system is globally asymptotically stable. “Stable focus” and “stable node” both have that property. A center is Lyapunov stable but not asymptotically stable. A saddle remains unstable even when an initial state lies exactly on its decaying eigenvector: an arbitrarily small component in the other eigenvector eventually grows.

Repeated eigenvalues need care. At k=1,c=2k=1,c=2k=1,c=2, solutions include te−tte^{-t}te−t as well as e−te^{-t}e−t, and both decay. A repeated zero eigenvalue need not behave that way. A six-unit plot illustrates finite-time motion; eigenvalue analysis establishes the asymptotic conclusion.

Why zero damping produces a center

For k>0k>0k>0, define a positive quadratic energy-like quantity

E(x,v)=12v2+12kx2,E˙=vv˙+kxx˙=−cv2.E(x,v)=\frac12v^2+\frac12kx^2, \qquad \dot E=v\dot v+kx\dot x=-cv^2.E(x,v)=21​v2+21​kx2,E˙=vv˙+kxx˙=−cv2.

When c=0c=0c=0, EEE is conserved. Nonzero initial states follow v2+kx2=2E0v^2+kx^2=2E_0v2+kx2=2E0​, so the orbits are ellipses. With k=1k=1k=1 and equal coordinate scales they are circles. Conservation keeps nearby states bounded but supplies no mechanism for their convergence to the origin. These closed orbits are a family of conservative motions, not an isolated attracting limit cycle.

Positive ccc removes this energy; negative ccc adds it. Energy may have zero instantaneous derivative when v=0v=0v=0 even with positive damping, so that one instant does not imply an equilibrium. The eigenvalue result establishes eventual convergence for k>0,c>0k>0,c>0k>0,c>0.

Zero stiffness: a line of equilibria

When k=0k=0k=0, every (x,0)(x,0)(x,0) is an equilibrium. For c>0c>0c>0,

v(t)=v0e−ct,x(t)=x0+v0c(1−e−ct),x(∞)=x0+v0c.v(t)=v_0e^{-ct},\qquad x(t)=x_0+\frac{v_0}{c}(1-e^{-ct}),\qquad x(\infty)=x_0+\frac{v_0}{c}.v(t)=v0​e−ct,x(t)=x0​+cv0​​(1−e−ct),x(∞)=x0​+cv0​​.

Velocity decays, but position generally settles at a different point on the line. Each equilibrium is Lyapunov stable; no individual point is asymptotically stable because nearby stationary positions remain where they started. Use k=0,c=1,x0=1,v0=1k=0,c=1,x_0=1,v_0=1k=0,c=1,x0​=1,v0​=1: the state tends to (2,0)(2,0)(2,0), rather than the origin.

At k=c=0k=c=0k=c=0, v(t)=v0v(t)=v_0v(t)=v0​ and x(t)=x0+v0tx(t)=x_0+v_0tx(t)=x0​+v0​t. Arbitrarily small nonzero velocity eventually causes arbitrarily large position drift, so an individual equilibrium is unstable. Both eigenvalues are zero, but the matrix has a nontrivial Jordan block. With k=0,c<0k=0,c<0k=0,c<0, nonzero velocity grows exponentially. A zero real part or the informal label “marginal” is therefore insufficient to claim stability.

Can trajectories cross?

Two position–time curves can intersect because their velocities can differ at the same position and time. For k=1,c=0k=1,c=0k=1,c=0, the motions xA=cos⁡tx_A=\cos txA​=cost and xB=sin⁡tx_B=\sin txB​=sint have equal position at t=π/4t=\pi/4t=π/4, but velocities −1/2-1/\sqrt2−1/2​ and +1/2+1/\sqrt2+1/2​ respectively. Their full states are different.

For this autonomous linear ODE, a full state uniquely determines the future. Distinct phase-plane orbits cannot cross transversely. Two solutions can trace the same orbit at different time offsets, and a periodic solution revisits its own state. Apparent crossings in an incomplete projection, coarse line sampling, or plots of different parameter settings do not violate uniqueness.

Connect the geometry to control

If the normalized plant instead had v˙=−k0x−c0v+u\dot v=-k_0x-c_0v+uv˙=−k0​x−c0​v+u and ideal feedback u=−Kpx−Kdvu=-K_px-K_dvu=−Kp​x−Kd​v, its closed-loop coefficients would be k=k0+Kpk=k_0+K_pk=k0​+Kp​ and c=c0+Kdc=c_0+K_dc=c0​+Kd​. Feedback changes the field, whereas changing the initial state selects a different solution in the same field. This relation assumes continuous, instantaneous state feedback with unlimited input.

The explorer itself is unforced and linear. It includes no delay, sampling, force limits, contact, nonlinear friction or measured human motion. A linearization of a physical system supports local conclusions around its operating point; a large excursion may leave that approximation. Oral practice

Continue to the Stability and Performance explorer to test a third-order feedback system by moving its gain across the imaginary-axis crossing. Compare a forced response with decay from a nonzero initial state.

  • Does changing the initial state change the eigenvalues? No. It changes modal amplitudes; AAA and its eigenvalues stay fixed.
  • Does a node mean every position trace is monotone? No. Suitable initial velocity can cause a turning point or a sign change even with real negative eigenvalues. The worked example has no overshoot because of its particular initial state.
  • Why is the center not asymptotically stable? Nonzero initial energy remains on a nonzero closed orbit.
  • Why can a decaying saddle trajectory be misleading? It may lie on the stable eigenvector while nearby trajectories contain a growing mode.
  • Does a trajectory leaving the figure hit a physical boundary? No. The viewing window clips the drawing; the model continues evolving.

Check your understanding

  • Distinguish a direction field, an individual trajectory and time traces.
  • Predict node, focus, center and saddle behavior from the model's eigenvalues.
  • Check an exact trajectory against its initial state and differential equation.
  • Explain why zero damping and zero stiffness require separate stability arguments.
  • Recognize normalization, axis scaling and finite display limits.
