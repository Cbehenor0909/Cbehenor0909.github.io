Center of Pressure · Contact Forces and Plate Wrenches

Replace distributed ground contact by a resultant force, locate its center of pressure, and keep force-plate origins and sign conventions explicit.

intermediateFunctionalReading lesson

Where does the resultant ground force act, and why is that point different from the center of mass?

Contact has a location; mass has another

Stand on one foot and change how strongly the heel and toes press. The foot outline can stay fixed while the resultant contact force shifts. That moving application point is the center of pressure, or COP. The center of mass, or COM, comes from the body’s mass distribution. Their definitions use different weights and need not give the same horizontal location.

Read the diagram. Red arrows represent contact forces on the body. Their vertical components combine at the teal cross; the filled ochre point belongs to body mass. In the lower side view, the plate’s wrench origin lies below the surface. Moving a moment to the surface changes the moment components even though the physical contact stays unchanged. Geometry is schematic; no measured recording is shown.

For nonnegative normal forces fz,if_{z,i}fz,i​ on a flat surface,

px=∑ixifz,i∑ifz,i,py=∑iyifz,i∑ifz,i.p_x=\frac{\sum_i x_i f_{z,i}}{\sum_i f_{z,i}},\qquad p_y=\frac{\sum_i y_i f_{z,i}}{\sum_i f_{z,i}}.px​=∑i​fz,i​∑i​xi​fz,i​​,py​=∑i​fz,i​∑i​yi​fz,i​​.

A pressure field uses the corresponding area integrals. This weighted average lies inside the convex hull of the loaded contact locations. It does not identify which individual foot produced each contribution. Compare the mass weighting in Center of Mass and the active-contact geometry in Base of Support.

State the wrench convention before dividing

A six-component force plate provides three forces and three moments, from which COP is calculated; see Bertec’s channel description. Here use a right-handed plate frame with zzz upward, wrench origin OOO, and contact plane z=hz=hz=h. Both F\mathbf FF and MO\mathbf M_OMO​ describe the plate’s action on the body. A manufacturer’s raw channels may use the opposite action or different axes; convert the whole wrench first.

Write rOP=[px,py,h]T\mathbf r_{OP}=[p_x,p_y,h]^\mathsf TrOP​=[px​,py​,h]T. For contact whose residual couple is purely normal to the plane,

MO=rOP×F+Tzz^.\mathbf M_O=\mathbf r_{OP}\times\mathbf F+T_z\hat{\mathbf z}.MO​=rOP​×F+Tz​z^.

The first two components give

Mx=pyFz−hFy,py=Mx+hFyFz,My=hFx−pxFz,px=hFx−MyFz.\begin{aligned} M_x&=p_yF_z-hF_y, & p_y&=\frac{M_x+hF_y}{F_z},\\ M_y&=hF_x-p_xF_z, & p_x&=\frac{hF_x-M_y}{F_z}. \end{aligned}Mx​My​​=py​Fz​−hFy​,=hFx​−px​Fz​,​py​px​​=Fz​Mx​+hFy​​,=Fz​hFx​−My​​.​

The remaining free moment is Tz=Mz−pxFy+pyFxT_z=M_z-p_xF_y+p_yF_xTz​=Mz​−px​Fy​+py​Fx​. At h=0h=0h=0, the familiar expressions are px=−My/Fzp_x=-M_y/F_zpx​=−My​/Fz​ and py=Mx/Fzp_y=M_x/F_zpy​=Mx​/Fz​. These signs follow our stated cross product, not a universal vendor convention. Forces use newtons; moments use newton metres; hhh and COP use metres. Vicon’s setup documentation explains why origin, position and orientation all matter.

Combining contacts and rejecting unloaded samples

For two coplanar plates, transform both COPs and vertical forces into one ground frame, then use p=(Fz,1p1+Fz,2p2)/(Fz,1+Fz,2)\mathbf p=(F_{z,1}\mathbf p_1+F_{z,2}\mathbf p_2)/(F_{z,1}+F_{z,2})p=(Fz,1​p1​+Fz,2​p2​)/(Fz,1​+Fz,2​). For other geometries, transform and sum the complete wrenches about a common origin before choosing a plane. A force vector rotates; a moment also changes when its reference origin changes.

Division becomes ill-conditioned as FzF_zFz​ approaches zero. Declare a load threshold based on the instrument and analysis purpose, mark unloaded COP as unavailable, and inspect saturation and baseline offsets. An arbitrary position at zero load is not a valid contact estimate. Human Motion Sensing connects these checks to synchronized kinematics; Dynamic Balance adds COM velocity.

Common mistakes

  • Calling COP the projection of COM, or inferring COM from COP alone.
  • Mixing moments in N mm\mathrm{N\,mm}Nmm with offsets in metres.
  • Using a surface-origin formula with a subsurface wrench origin.
  • Drawing a swing foot as loaded support or plotting COP through zero-force gaps.

Oral practice

  1. Explain why shifting toe pressure can move COP without moving the foot outline.
  2. Derive pxp_xpx​ from the yyy component of r×F\mathbf r\times\mathbf Fr×F.
  3. Describe how you would recognize an invalid COP sample and combine two plates.

Check your understanding

  • Distinguish a mass-weighted COM from a pressure-weighted COP.
  • Derive COP from a six-component wrench with a surface-height offset.
  • Transform plate coordinates before combining contacts.
  • Recognize unloaded-contact and force-threshold limitations.
