Generalized Coordinates and Degrees of Freedom

Count independent configuration variables, express constraints, and map joint motion into Cartesian position, velocity and acceleration.

intermediateFunctionalReading lesson

How many independent numbers describe a mechanism's configuration, and how do their derivatives generate motion?

Coordinates should describe independent motion

A free particle needs three position coordinates in space. A free rigid body needs three for translation and three local orientation parameters: six degrees of freedom. A planar rigid body needs xxx, yyy and one angle. These counts describe configuration, not the number of state variables required by a second-order dynamic model.

A generalized coordinate need not be a Cartesian distance. An angle, cable displacement or slider extension can be a convenient coordinate if the set determines configuration locally. A grounded open chain with three independent single-axis revolute joints ordinarily has three degrees of freedom, although every link has a 3-D pose. Joint constraints couple those poses.

For nnn candidate coordinates x\mathbf xx and kkk independent holonomic constraints ϕ(x,t)=0\boldsymbol\phi(\mathbf x,t)=\mathbf0ϕ(x,t)=0, the local configuration dimension is

n−rank⁡(∂ϕ∂x).n-\operatorname{rank}\left(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol\phi}{\partial\mathbf x}\right).n−rank(∂x∂ϕ​).

Counting equations alone is insufficient: constraints may be redundant, and rank can change at singular configurations. State the geometry and operating configuration before applying a mobility formula.

Position constraints and velocity constraints

A pendulum particle with fixed length lll satisfies x2+y2=l2x^2+y^2=l^2x2+y2=l2. One angle qqq can replace two coordinates and this constraint. A constraint with no explicit time dependence is scleronomic; a moving support can make it time dependent.

Differentiating a holonomic constraint gives an admissible-velocity condition:

∂ϕ∂xx˙+∂ϕ∂t=0.\frac{\partial\boldsymbol\phi}{\partial\mathbf x}\dot{\mathbf x} +\frac{\partial\boldsymbol\phi}{\partial t}=\mathbf0.∂x∂ϕ​x˙+∂t∂ϕ​=0.

Not every velocity constraint integrates into a position constraint. A wheel rolling in a fixed straight line has an integrable relation x−Rθ=constantx-R\theta=\text{constant}x−Rθ=constant under the chosen no-slip convention. A wheel allowed to steer in a plane has nonholonomic no-slip constraints; it cannot generally be reduced by inventing an equivalent algebraic relation between all configuration coordinates.

Generalized speeds u\mathbf uu are another choice of velocity variables. A model may use q˙=N(q)u\dot{\mathbf q}=N(\mathbf q)\mathbf uq˙​=N(q)u rather than u=q˙\mathbf u=\dot{\mathbf q}u=q˙​. This matters for Euler angles and multibody software. Declare the mapping rather than equating names.

Position maps become velocity maps

For a time-independent point map r=r(q)\mathbf r=\mathbf r(\mathbf q)r=r(q),

J(q)=∂r∂q,v=Jq˙,a=Jq¨+J˙q˙.\begin{aligned} J(\mathbf q)&=\frac{\partial\mathbf r}{\partial\mathbf q},\\ \mathbf v&=J\dot{\mathbf q},\\ \mathbf a&=J\ddot{\mathbf q}+\dot J\dot{\mathbf q}. \end{aligned}J(q)va​=∂q∂r​,=Jq˙​,=Jq¨​+J˙q˙​.​

The J˙q˙\dot J\dot{\mathbf q}J˙q˙​ term contains acceleration caused by changing geometry, even at constant coordinate rates. If the map depends explicitly on time, add ∂r/∂t\partial\mathbf r/\partial t∂r/∂t to velocity and the corresponding mixed/time derivatives to acceleration. Angular velocity also has a configuration-dependent map; its Jacobian is distinct from a point’s translational Jacobian.

Zero joint angular accelerations therefore do not imply zero tip acceleration. This result is the planar counterpart of Multi-Link Transport. The example prescribes motion; it does not determine the joint torques needed to produce it.

Oral checks and common error

Common error: counting every Cartesian point coordinate as an independent degree of freedom, or dropping J˙q˙\dot J\dot{\mathbf q}J˙q˙​ when rates are constant.

  • How many state variables does a regular two-coordinate mechanical model need? Usually four: two coordinates and two independent speeds, with consistent initial conditions.
  • Is a Jacobian singularity necessarily a loss of mechanism mobility? No. A point task can lose instantaneous directions while the joints remain independently movable; distinguish the task Jacobian from constraint rank.
  • Why choose minimal coordinates? They satisfy position constraints by construction and can remove ideal reaction forces from the motion equations.

Use Equations of Motion to turn these coordinates into a dynamic model. The configuration-counting foundation is also developed in the MIT degrees-of-freedom notes.

Check your understanding

  • Distinguish configuration coordinates, independent degrees of freedom and state variables.
  • Recognize holonomic constraints and velocity constraints.
  • Compute Cartesian kinematics from a coordinate map and its Jacobian.
