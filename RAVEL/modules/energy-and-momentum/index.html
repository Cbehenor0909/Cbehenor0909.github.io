Energy, Work and Momentum

Decide which conservation law applies by inspecting external forces, moments and power, then use energy and momentum to check a dynamic model.

intermediateFunctionalReading lesson

What can be conserved even when a body accelerates, and what physical condition justifies that conservation?

Three balances, three different conditions

Energy conservation, linear momentum conservation and angular momentum conservation are different statements. A body may conserve one while changing the others. Begin with a chosen system boundary, an inertial frame, a reference point for moments, and the loads crossing that boundary.

For constant masses, total linear momentum is P=MvC\mathbf P=M\mathbf v_CP=MvC​, and

N ⁣dPdt=∑Fext.{}^N\!\frac{d\mathbf P}{dt}=\sum\mathbf F_{\mathrm{ext}}.NdtdP​=∑Fext​.

Zero net external force conserves the vector P\mathbf PP even if internal motions change. Integrating over time gives impulse–momentum: ΔP=∫∑Fextdt\Delta\mathbf P=\int\sum\mathbf F_{\mathrm{ext}}dtΔP=∫∑Fext​dt. A short collision can permit neglecting some external impulses; justify the time-scale approximation rather than declaring all forces zero.

HO=∑irOi×mivi,N ⁣dHOdt=∑τO,ext.\begin{aligned} \mathbf H_O&=\sum_i\mathbf r_{Oi}\times m_i\mathbf v_i,\\ {}^N\!\frac{d\mathbf H_O}{dt}&=\sum\boldsymbol\tau_{O,\mathrm{ext}}. \end{aligned}HO​NdtdHO​​​=i∑​rOi​×mi​vi​,=∑τO,ext​.​

Zero net external moment about that point conserves HO\mathbf H_OHO​; a zero moment about some other point is not interchangeable. For a rigid body, HO=rOC×MvC+ICω\mathbf H_O=\mathbf r_{OC}\times M\mathbf v_C+\mathsf I_C\boldsymbol\omegaHO​=rOC​×MvC​+IC​ω. The first term is orbital momentum and the second is spin. Angular momentum can be constant in the inertial frame while its body components change.

Kinetic energy and work

Dotting a particle’s Newton equation with velocity gives dT/dt=F⋅vdT/dt=\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf vdT/dt=F⋅v. For a rigid body,

T=12MvC⋅vC+12ω⋅ICω.T=\frac12M\mathbf v_C\cdot\mathbf v_C +\frac12\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\mathsf I_C\boldsymbol\omega.T=21​MvC​⋅vC​+21​ω⋅IC​ω.

Use centroidal inertia here. Replacing it with inertia about an arbitrary point double counts or omits translation unless the kinetic-energy expression is also changed consistently. Total kinetic energy is the sum over bodies.

The power of an applied force is F⋅vP\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf v_PF⋅vP​ at its application point PPP, and a couple supplies τ⋅ω\boldsymbol\tau\cdot\boldsymbol\omegaτ⋅ω. Work is the time integral of power. Large force does not automatically mean large work: a force perpendicular to velocity has zero instantaneous power.

Potential and nonconservative effects

A conservative force admits a potential, F=−∇V\mathbf F=-\nabla VF=−∇V, on the domain of interest. Its work depends on endpoints rather than path. Uniform gravity gives V=MgyCV=Mgy_CV=MgyC​ with upward yyy; a linear spring gives V=kx2/2V=kx^2/2V=kx2/2 relative to its unstretched length. The zero of potential is arbitrary, but its derivatives are not.

For time-independent conservative potentials, including all remaining force and couple power in PncP_{\mathrm{nc}}Pnc​,

d(T+V)dt=Pnc.\frac{d(T+V)}{dt}=P_{\mathrm{nc}}.dtd(T+V)​=Pnc​.

Viscous damping dissipates power −bq˙2-b\dot q^2−bq˙​2; an actuator can add or remove energy. Moving supports can also do work. With explicitly time-dependent potential, add ∂V/∂t\partial V/\partial t∂V/∂t to the balance. Mechanical energy is conserved only when these energy-transfer terms vanish. Internal forces can redistribute or store energy even though their net force cancels.

Oral checks and common error

Common error: assuming that every collision conserves kinetic energy, or inferring all conservation laws from the absence of friction.

  • Can angular momentum stay constant while kinetic energy increases? Yes. Moving mass inward can decrease inertia while internal work increases kinetic energy at fixed angular momentum.
  • Does static friction always do zero work? No. It does here because the fixed-ramp contact point is instantaneously at rest; a moving surface can transfer energy.
  • What is conserved in an isolated perfectly inelastic collision? Linear momentum, while some kinetic energy becomes other forms; total energy still balances when those forms are included.

Use the MIT work–energy notes for further examples, then Equations of Motion to derive dynamics from TTT and VVV.

Check your understanding

  • Calculate translational and rotational kinetic energy with a consistent inertia.
  • State distinct conditions for energy, linear momentum and angular momentum conservation.
  • Apply work–energy and impulse–momentum balances to worked examples.
