Robot Dynamics and Actuation

Separate inertia, velocity coupling, gravity and contact loads, then combine inverse-dynamics feedforward with feedback and actuator limits.

intermediateFunctionalReading lesson

Which torques are required to create a desired motion, and which effects can invalidate that prediction?

Motion needs effort

Kinematics says where the robot can move. Dynamics says how its motion changes under effort. A prescribed human-derived trajectory supplies a reference; it does not automatically supply feasible robot torques. Accelerating a heavy link, resisting gravity and pushing an object are distinct demands.

Read the diagram. Gravity acts at the center of mass, while motor torque acts at the joint. The angle is measured from downward vertical, so a positive angle requires a positive holding torque in the convention below. The rod is a one-joint example, not a complete robot model.

For all-revolute coordinates, effort components have units N m\mathrm{N\,m}Nm and mass-matrix entries kg m2\mathrm{kg\,m^2}kgm2. A prismatic coordinate instead has a force as its conjugate effort. Every term must match the units of its row; mixed-joint matrices cannot be assigned one uniform scalar unit.

Feedforward is not exact cancellation

Feedforward evaluated along the desired trajectory predicts nominal effort; feedback responds to measured deviations. Full computed torque instead evaluates the modeled nonlinear terms at the current state and commands

τ=M(q)(q¨d+Kve˙+Kae)+Cq˙+g.\boldsymbol\tau=M(\mathbf q) \left(\ddot{\mathbf q}_d+K_v\dot{\mathbf e}+K_a\mathbf e\right) +C\dot{\mathbf q}+\mathbf g.τ=M(q)(q¨​d​+Kv​e˙+Ka​e)+Cq˙​+g.

With exact modeling, direct ideal torque actuation, no external load and no saturation, substitution gives e¨+Kve˙+Kae=0\ddot{\mathbf e}+K_v\dot{\mathbf e}+K_a\mathbf e=\mathbf0e¨+Kv​e˙+Ka​e=0. This displayed law uses the friction-free general plant; applying it to the viscous rod above also requires compensation bq˙b\dot qbq˙​ at the current velocity. These acceleration-level gains have units s−1\mathrm{s^{-1}}s−1 and s−2\mathrm{s^{-2}}s−2, unlike the torque-level gains above. Model error, elastic transmissions and sampled feedback weaken this cancellation. See Computed-Torque Control.

In contact, constraint reactions must be solved with admissible accelerations; they cannot be chosen independently of geometry. Gear ratios, rotor inertia, friction, motor-current loops and effort limits also belong to the actuator model when relevant. Constrained Dynamics develops the contact constraint formulation.

Oral checks and common errors

  • Forward or inverse dynamics? Forward predicts acceleration from effort; inverse predicts effort for a specified acceleration and state.
  • Why compensate gravity? It supplies holding effort even at zero tracking error.
  • Does larger feedback gain ensure better tracking? It may demand unavailable effort and amplify noisy or delayed estimates.

Common errors: reversing the external-force sign, confusing torque gains with acceleration gains, or calling a simulated command hardware validation. Continue to Sampled Feedback and Impedance and Admittance for timing and interaction effects.

Check your understanding

  • Explain the physical meaning and units of the manipulator equation.
  • Distinguish forward dynamics, inverse dynamics and feedback correction.
  • Calculate a gravity-loaded joint's feedforward and PD effort.
  • Identify how friction, contact constraints and effort limits change the model.
