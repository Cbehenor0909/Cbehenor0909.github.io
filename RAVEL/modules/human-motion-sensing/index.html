Human Motion Sensing · Measurements to Model States

Choose sensing modalities by the quantities they observe, align frames and clocks, and separate raw channels from estimated motion and control states.

introductoryFunctionalReading lesson

Which quantities are observed by each sensor, and what assumptions turn them into states a controller can use?

Start with the quantity you need

“Measure movement” can mean position, orientation, contact force, muscle electrical activity or an estimated joint coordinate. These quantities are related, but each sensor observes only part of the chain. A controller that needs joint position and velocity cannot use every channel as if it were already that state.

Read the diagram. Teal outlines mark observation channels; blue outlines mark derived states and control computations. The calibration block supplies frame, unit and time meaning before channels are compared. The quality gate can mark a sample unavailable. The final action changes the body that sensors observe, closing the loop. This is an explanatory architecture, not an implemented hardware connection.

Observed channels and inferred quantities

Modality Observed or reconstructed quantity Additional inference and important limit
IMU Sensor-frame specific force, angular rate; possibly magnetic field Orientation is estimated; position and anatomical joint angle are not raw channels
Marker-based mocap Camera detections and calibrated 3-D marker positions Segment pose and joint coordinates require marker assignment and a model; skin motion can alter them
Force plate Calibrated force and moment channels COP follows a wrench convention and sufficient load; individual muscle forces are not supplied
Surface EMG Electrical potential differences at skin electrodes Envelope/normalized features describe electrical activity; activation and muscle force require further modeling
Video or depth camera Image intensities; calibrated depth when available Keypoints and 3-D pose are algorithmic estimates affected by occlusion, calibration and viewing geometry

VectorNav describes inertial channels, and Delsys explains the electrical origin and placement dependence of surface EMG. EMG amplitude is not a direct force transducer: muscle length, contraction dynamics, recruitment and electrode placement affect the relationship. Vicon describes image keypoint detection followed by multi-view skeletal reconstruction. A reconstructed joint center is not a directly observed anatomical landmark.

Declare angle units, length units, axis handedness and matrix direction. A gyroscope value in degrees per second must become radians per second before it enters a radians-based kinematic model. World orientation and a fixed mounting offset are distinct calibration quantities.

Resample onto a declared common timeline after accounting for offsets, drift and acquisition latency. Interpolation adds no missing information; avoid silently spanning long gaps. Distinguish event timestamp from packet-arrival time. Signal Processing and State Estimation treats sampling, noise and derivative estimates.

From estimates to useful actions

Check missing samples, clipping, baseline bias, tracking loss, sensor motion relative to skin and calibration validity. Keep raw channels and derived values labeled separately. In OpenSim inverse kinematics, generalized coordinates fit model markers to observed trajectories; fitting error should be inspected rather than treating output angles as exact.

For control, carry each estimate’s timestamp, availability and relevant uncertainty into the decision. An old estimate can produce a different action from a current one. The Sensing–Control Lab illustrates this link with synthetic states; it does not connect devices or validate anatomical measurements.

From a sensing method to published evidence

Wade et al. (2022) provide a scoping review of markerless gait methods. Use it to identify tasks, camera arrangements, reference methods and unresolved limitations before choosing a pipeline. A review organizes evidence; it does not make every pose estimate interchangeable with anatomical kinematics.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a quaternion, camera pose or joint angle as an unprocessed sensor channel.
  • Calling specific force gravity-free acceleration.
  • Matching array indices when sample clocks or rates differ.
  • Equating normalized EMG amplitude with normalized muscle force.

Oral practice

  1. For a joint-angle controller, name the measurements and inference steps you need.
  2. Explain the stationary accelerometer reading and the role of orientation.
  3. Describe what you would check before combining a force plate with motion capture.

Check your understanding

  • Compare inertial, optical, force, electrical and image measurements.
  • Distinguish raw channels, reconstructed geometry and inferred states.
  • Declare coordinate-frame, unit and clock contracts.
  • Trace calibration and quality checks through estimation to control.
