Inertia Scalars, Dyadics and Tensors

Build inertia from a mass distribution, find the inertia about an axis, and transform or shift its matrix without losing frame and point labels.

advancedFunctionalReading lesson

Why can angular momentum point in a different direction from angular velocity?

Mass resists translation; its distribution resists rotation

Two bodies can have the same mass and center of mass but different resistance to rotation. Moving material farther from an axis increases the moment of inertia about that axis. An inertia value is incomplete unless its reference point and axis are specified.

Let ρ\boldsymbol\rhoρ point from reference point OOO to an element of mass. The inertia dyadic is the coordinate-independent linear map

IO=∫[(ρ⋅ρ)1−ρ⊗ρ]dm.\mathsf I_O=\int\left[(\boldsymbol\rho\cdot\boldsymbol\rho)\mathsf1 -\boldsymbol\rho\otimes\boldsymbol\rho\right]dm.IO​=∫[(ρ⋅ρ)1−ρ⊗ρ]dm.

Here 1\mathsf11 is the identity map. The dyadic product acts as (ρ⊗ρ)a=ρ(ρ⋅a)(\boldsymbol\rho\otimes\boldsymbol\rho)\mathbf a=\boldsymbol\rho(\boldsymbol\rho\cdot\mathbf a)(ρ⊗ρ)a=ρ(ρ⋅a). A tensor matrix is this map represented in a chosen basis; it is not a separate physical property.

Matrix entries and axis inertia

Using ρ=[x,y,z]T\boldsymbol\rho=[x,y,z]^Tρ=[x,y,z]T in an orthonormal basis,

IO=[∫(y2+z2)dm−∫xy dm−∫xz dm−∫xy dm∫(x2+z2)dm−∫yz dm−∫xz dm−∫yz dm∫(x2+y2)dm].I_O=\begin{bmatrix} \int(y^2+z^2)dm&-\int xy\,dm&-\int xz\,dm\\ -\int xy\,dm&\int(x^2+z^2)dm&-\int yz\,dm\\ -\int xz\,dm&-\int yz\,dm&\int(x^2+y^2)dm \end{bmatrix}.IO​=​∫(y2+z2)dm−∫xydm−∫xzdm​−∫xydm∫(x2+z2)dm−∫yzdm​−∫xzdm−∫yzdm∫(x2+y2)dm​​.

The units are kg m2\mathrm{kg\,m^2}kgm2. Here the negative signs are included in the off-diagonal matrix entries. Some books define a positive product of inertia Pxy=∫xy dmP_{xy}=\int xy\,dmPxy​=∫xydm and place −Pxy-P_{xy}−Pxy​ in the matrix. Check the convention before copying values into software.

For a unit direction e^\hat{\mathbf e}e^ through OOO, the scalar moment is

IO,e^=e^TIOe^=∫∥ρ×e^∥2dm.I_{O,\hat{\mathbf e}}=\hat{\mathbf e}^{T}I_O\hat{\mathbf e} =\int\lVert\boldsymbol\rho\times\hat{\mathbf e}\rVert^2dm.IO,e^​=e^TIO​e^=∫∥ρ×e^∥2dm.

It is nonnegative. An ideal line distribution can have a zero eigenvalue about its own line, so positive definiteness is not automatic for every idealized geometry. Principal axes are orthonormal eigenvectors of the symmetric inertia matrix; in that basis the matrix is diagonal. Reflection symmetry often makes particular products vanish, but symmetry must be established about the stated axes.

Rotate a basis; shift a reference point

For RNBR_{NB}RNB​ mapping body components into NNN,

ION=RNBIOBRNBT.I_O^N=R_{NB}I_O^B R_{NB}^{T}.ION​=RNB​IOB​RNBT​.

The mass distribution is unchanged. The components change because the basis changes. A rigid body’s centroidal inertia is constant in its body basis, while its representation in Ground generally changes with orientation.

For d\mathbf dd from the COM CCC to OOO, with every quantity expressed in one basis, the parallel-axis theorem is

IO=IC+M[(d⋅d)1−ddT].I_O=I_C+M\left[(\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf d)1-\mathbf d\mathbf d^T\right].IO​=IC​+M[(d⋅d)1−ddT].

Projecting this matrix onto an axis gives IO,e^=IC,e^+Md⊥2I_{O,\hat{\mathbf e}}=I_{C,\hat{\mathbf e}}+Md_\perp^2IO,e^​=IC,e^​+Md⊥2​, where the two axes are parallel. The full distance ∥d∥\lVert\mathbf d\rVert∥d∥ is correct only when the displacement is perpendicular to the axis. Shifting between two arbitrary non-centroidal points requires first recovering the centroidal tensor or retaining the first-moment terms.

Oral checks and common error

Common error: using one scalar inertia in a general spatial equation, or shifting an inertia that was already about the desired point.

  • When are angular momentum and angular velocity parallel? When angular velocity lies along a principal axis, including any direction in a repeated-eigenvalue eigenspace.
  • Does a tensor change when I rotate the coordinate axes? Its matrix representation changes; the physical linear map does not.
  • What quick checks catch a bad inertia matrix? Symmetry, units, nonnegative axis moments and appropriate principal-moment inequalities for a physical mass distribution.

The MIT inertia-tensor notes provide further spatial examples. Use Energy and Momentum to connect this tensor to rotational kinetic energy.

Check your understanding

  • Construct an inertia dyadic and its symmetric matrix representation.
  • Distinguish moments about axes from products of inertia.
  • Apply rotation and parallel-axis transformations with explicit conventions.
