Shifts and convolution

L{eatf(t)}=F(s−a).\mathcal{L}\{e^{at}f(t)\}=F(s-a).L{eatf(t)}=F(s−a).

Limits are conditional statements

For a regular signal with a finite right limit and suitable transform behavior,

f(0+)=lim⁡s→+∞sF(s).f(0^+)=\lim_{s\to+\infty}sF(s).f(0+)=s→+∞lim​sF(s).

This initial value theorem does not interpret impulsive outputs as ordinary finite values. For the step displacement, the limit is zero.

For rational transforms of regular causal signals, a useful sufficient condition for the final value theorem is that every pole of sF(s)sF(s)sF(s) lies strictly in the left half-plane, after exact algebraic cancellation:

Oral practice

  • Why does an initial displacement appear in the numerator? It is a separate source of response introduced by the derivative transform.
  • Does a finite lim⁡s→0sF(s)\lim_{s\to0}sF(s)lims→0​sF(s) guarantee a final value? No; stability or convergence conditions must also hold.
  • What is special about s=jωs=j\omegas=jω? For a stable LTI system it evaluates sinusoidal steady-state gain and phase, connecting Laplace analysis to Bode loop shaping.
