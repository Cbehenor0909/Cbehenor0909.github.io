Poles, zeros and hidden dynamics

Poles of a reduced rational transfer function are singularities in its input/output relation. Zeros are frequencies where that relation vanishes, subject to scalar or matrix zero definitions. For a controllable and observable finite-dimensional realization, the transfer poles coincide with the state eigenvalues, including multiplicity.

They need not coincide for a nonminimal realization. For example,

A rational transfer is proper when numerator degree does not exceed denominator degree; it is strictly proper when the degree is smaller. A standard finite-dimensional ODE state model with ordinary input and finite DDD has a proper transfer. Oral practice

  • Does G(0)G(0)G(0) always predict the final output? Only when the relevant response converges. For a stable step response, DC gain gives its final scaling.
  • Can two state models have the same GGG? Yes. Similarity transformations preserve GGG; extra uncontrollable or unobservable states can also leave it unchanged.
  • Why specify zero initial conditions? They remove the independent response from stored state, allowing the input/output relation to be written as multiplication by GGG alone.

Use feedback interconnections to combine these models, and controllability and observability to examine dynamics hidden from the transfer function.

  • Relate impulse response to convolution and direct feedthrough.
  • Distinguish transfer-function poles from all state eigenvalues.
  • Interpret properness and physical output units.
