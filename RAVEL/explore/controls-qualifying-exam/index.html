Controls Qualifying Exam

Nine outline topics connect linear algebra and ODEs to state space, Laplace methods, feedback, root locus and Bode design.

The supplied Controls Oral Qualifying Exam Concepts outline lists nine fundamental topic entries. This map gives every entry an available lesson and worked example.

Follow the Controls study path, or open the topic you need. A consistent mass-spring-damper and double-integrator vocabulary makes it easier to compare time-domain, Laplace-domain, frequency-domain and state-space answers.

Explain the same system four ways

Use the Phase Portraits explorer beside the ODE, state-space and stability lessons. Its normalized second-order model connects the eigenvalues to a direction field and matching position/velocity traces.

The Stability and Performance explorer continues that comparison with a cubic feedback model, a Routh calculation and two response experiments at the same gain.

Continue to Root Locus to reveal all three branches for the same cubic, distinguish breakaway from the stability boundary and test which desired poles a positive real gain can reach.

An ODE states a dynamical relationship. State space specifies memory, inputs and outputs. A transfer function describes zero-initial-state input/output behavior. Frequency response describes sinusoidal steady-state behavior where that response exists. Moving between these descriptions requires their assumptions, including causality, initial conditions and stability.

Practice design as a chain of arguments

Declare the feedback sign, model and performance target. Propose a controller, calculate the resulting dynamics, and check stability before quoting settling time or steady-state error. Use the mechanics-to-controls example to practice this chain with a model derived from physical forces.
